## Plot Events

### Wards of St. Olaf's
The session opened mid-infiltration: the party, disguised as orphans from the fictitious "St. Olaf's Home for Abandoned Youth," approached the barracks compound under the escort of a nun-costumed chaperone. Elspeth, playing the seasoned guardian shepherding her wards, talked the group past the gate with a deception check of **30** — smooth enough that the gate guard, a friendly soul the party nicknamed Derek (or Samwise, depending on who was asking), handed over two gold pieces for snacks and singled out the "super sad one" for special sympathy. That distinction belonged to Leliana, freshly returned to the table after an absence and fully committed to an eight-year-old's eye patch, raggedy clothes, and a pathetically homemade prosthetic leg. Bru, meanwhile, wore his Colonel Sanders finery *underneath* orphan rags — a costuming choice offered with total sincerity and received with equally total confusion.

Inside the gate, the group was invited to a concert in the barracks hall, thirty minutes out. Small talk drifted toward the party's assorted educations — monastery school, goblin school, a "hybrid prestigious" upbringing — with Silas landing a dark joke about child labor in the goblin mines that got the expected wince from the table. Elspeth, walking the guards' friendliness back to camp, admitted she didn't feel good about lying so effectively to fascists; the group half-joked about needing a support group once the bombs went off — an "explosion party," a "tech talk" support circle for people who blow things up for a living.

> "I'd love some popcorn, sir." — Silas

### The Concert That Wasn't a Concert
What awaited in the barracks hall was not entertainment. Musicians filed in with unnervingly synchronized movements and vacant eyes; shackled prisoners — clearly non-Eldoran, marked with religious tattoos and necklaces — were marched in alongside them. The party recognized the shape of the thing before the DM had to spell it out: this was a ritual, not a performance, using harmonic resonance to bridge the physical world to the divine. Mid-ceremony, a soldier ascended in front of the crowd — ephemeral white-gold wings unfurling, a summoned greatsword blazing into being — and the party watched an ordinary Eldoran trooper become something closer to an angel.

Recognizing the danger of the music itself, the party discreetly packed their ears with an improvised paste made from spare explosive charges, resisting checks along the way to avoid detection while doing so. Leliana, whose brother's death has haunted her since Session 58's revelations about Commander Steel, watched the ascension with a mixture of horror and envy that did not go unnoticed at the table.

> "I wish I had this— this is metal as hell with this goddamn wings." — Leliana

The implications sobered the group fast: this harmonic system — belief powering gods, gods empowering believers, an engine that only grows — might be the same architecture behind the Eldoran Empire's weapon in space. The concert hall itself, acoustically tuned and packed with worshippers, was reclassified on the spot from "venue" to "target," though the party held off on immediate demolition in favor of the coordinated multi-site strike already in motion.

### Popcorn Bags and Brick Walls
Their cover intact, the party charmed their way through to the end of the "show." Donald, the concert hall's evidently soft-hearted operator, was moved nearly to tears by the orphans' performance and had them smuggled out the back entrance stuffed into popcorn bags with breathing holes poked through the paper.

> "What a great performance. You kids really had a treat." — Donald

Outside, they found a fifteen-foot brick wall wrapped in heat-metal enchantment — arcana check **18** to identify — guarding the compound's rear approach, and spent a stretch of table time on increasingly baroque crossing schemes (levitation paired with a weightless-pee magic item drew comparisons to river-crossing riddles nobody asked for). With a workable plan shelved for later execution, the party shifted focus and disguises entirely, trading orphan rags for the look of bored college students en route to the university research campus.

Silas's hacky-sack routine — an acrobatics check of **32** — drew a small crowd of actual students, cover enough for the group to gather intel on the research towers' card-key access and surveillance routines. Leliana reached out to George via *message* spell, who declined to meet immediately and instead proposed a rendezvous later at the Sexy Peacock. Changing disguises again, this time into professional-looking researchers, the party cleared a run of stealth checks (**29, 24, 20, 17**) without raising an alarm.

### Bradley and Leah Go to Work
Silas lifted two employee key cards with a stealth check of **32**, and the party adopted the identities of "Bradley and Leah" to walk directly into the research facility. Choosing the quieter acoustics floor over the more heavily monitored experimentation level, the group's investigation rolls — **18, 19, 20, 20** — cracked the case wide open.

The findings were dense and disturbing: the Eldoran Empire's researchers had built out a full theory of **harmonic resonance**, using sound and music as the working proxy for how the physical, astral, and divine planes intertwine. A substance called **biolescence** enables that resonance, letting power be drawn directly from belief into the material world — the same mechanism, the party now understood, that had just turned a soldier into a winged knight in the barracks hall. Notes referenced studies of **Eichek**, a god of passion and music, as the original template: followers don't need explicit doctrine, only passionate practice, and the god grows regardless. The system creates a feedback loop — belief feeds the pantheon, the pantheon feeds the believer — that, left unchecked, runs away with itself. Tantalizingly, the same notes flagged **dragon orbs** as a possible disruption mechanism for exactly that kind of runaway loop.

Grimmer confirmation followed: experimentation on living subjects was underway on the top floor, and the building's layout — three towers, elevator shafts spanning all three — offered an obvious demolition plan, timed charges at the base of each shaft. Leliana, doing the math on what "disrupt the system" actually meant in practice, voiced the quiet part out loud.

> "I don't want to like blow up people. Isn't that weird?" — Leliana

The session's final revelation landed like a gift: the head scientist's wife is slated to attend Leliana's lunch event tomorrow. The party clocked it immediately as mission-critical and closed out the night preparing for the opportunity.

> "I'm going to assume that Commander Steel is a pomp as f*** and he would be in the center tower." — Silas

## Notable Character Moments
- **Elspeth Cooper** carried the barracks infiltration on a 30-point deception check, then admitted to the party that lying this well to fascists doesn't sit easy — a rare crack in her usually unshakeable competence.
- **Leliana Goldspring**, back at the table after a session away, threw herself fully into the sad-orphan disguise and then, minutes later, into genuine dread watching a soldier ascend the same way her brother's killer once might have — the callback to Commander Steel's Session 58 confirmation landing with real weight.
- **Bru** wore Colonel Sanders under orphan rags without a hint of self-consciousness, and somewhere in the barracks reportedly "lied his pants off" — literally, per the table's running joke.
- **Silas Fairbanks** turned in three exceptional rolls in one session (32 acrobatics, 32 stealth, plus a 30-range deception assist), cementing his role as the party's most reliable infiltration specialist — and drew the Archive's continued attention in the process.

## Themes
Infiltration and performance dominated the session — orphans, students, researchers, each identity a costume shed the moment it stopped being useful. Beneath the disguises sat a harder question about belief as fuel: the Eldoran Empire's entire military and divine apparatus runs on a feedback loop between worship and power, and the party is now standing directly inside the machinery, weighing how much of it can be broken without breaking the people trapped inside it. Leliana's quiet "isn't that weird?" about not wanting to kill people crystallized the session's moral center: sabotage is easy to plan in the abstract and much harder once the targets have faces.
