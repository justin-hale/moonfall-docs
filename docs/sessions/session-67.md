## Plot Events

### A Hologram Named Help

The table's ongoing war against the Eldoran Empire paused this week for what this correspondent can only describe as a *palate cleanser* — a small-batch, hand-pulled vignette, distinct in terroir from the grand continental sweep of recent sessions. It opened over brunch at a diner, where a radio advertisement for the Toyota Tacoma ("the engine warranty voids upon ignition") was interrupted by a Tacoma across the street catching fire and exploding, to no one's visible concern. Then came **Kevin**, the party's ever-reliable blur of a companion, arriving at speed bearing a flickering, static-choked holographic recording. The projection resolved into [Bru](/player-characters/bru), haggard, hunted, and unmistakably dramatic for the occasion:

> "Help me, Silas. You're my only hope."

Bru's message explained, in the clipped fashion of a man speaking quickly before his pursuers caught up, that ever since a Tacoma had blown up on the party's way to steal the mini-sun, he had been digging — and had, by his own account, blown up a couple of things and made himself known, and was now in hiding. His lead was a night-shift inspector named **Rivet**, fired for the crime of asking questions, who had hidden something at the **Toyota Works** factory in the worst part of town. If the party could get there, they might help him out — and get him out, before whoever was hunting him found him first.

### Into the Ruins of Toyota Works

The factory itself was a study in false fronts — a gleaming white glass atrium stitched awkwardly onto a rotting industrial hulk, its workers visibly miserable and smoking exclusively outdoors, as if the building itself discouraged joy from entering. [Leliana](/player-characters/leliana) took the sharpest first look (Perception 22) and picked out a receptionist through the front glass, feet up on the desk, nose in a magazine — while [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet), on a natural 1, was quite certain the place had been closed for years and that no one was there at all. Posing as corporate, come to throw the book at Rivet still harder, the trio approached the front desk, where the receptionist waved them off without ceremony:

> "That old Clanka, she got fired weeks ago, honey."

She pointed them toward Human Resources, and somewhere between the desk and the stairs, by psychic link, the cover names were settled: [Silas](/player-characters/silas) became "Kid Sour Patch," [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet) "Dr. Sour Straw," and [Leliana](/player-characters/leliana) "Dr. Warhead." On the way, [Silas](/player-characters/silas) studied the lobby directory (Perception 16) and found it a dreary roll call of corporate departments, broken only by a conspicuously placed "Marketing" office at Room 190 among the factory-floor rooms. [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet), meanwhile, looked out over the parking lot (Perception 24) and flagged two oddities worth a chronicler's italics: a tarped-over car tucked into an alley around the side, and the complete absence of any Toyota at all in the lot of a Toyota factory — Fords, Volkswagens, Chevys, but not one Tacoma — a detail made stranger by the world's long-running conviction that Tacoma owners are, as a class, perverts. Before leaving the atrium she fired off three *Message* spells toward the tarped car, behind the front desk, and into the lobby restroom, inviting Bru to answer; none came back.

At the door of Human Resources, the party did what Taco Cat does best in the absence of a better plan: they kicked it in. The office was dark and empty.

> *I am obligated to record that the receptionist's choice of slur for a peace-forged inspector went entirely unremarked upon by the party, who had larger doors to kick down. Some threads, alas, the Archive must leave dangling. — P.A.*

### Paper Trails and Pattern Firings

Rifling through a filing system clearly never organized by anyone who respected filing — [Silas](/player-characters/silas) tearing through the cabinets on Sleight of Hand 31 while his Investigation (7) and [Leliana](/player-characters/leliana)'s (a natural 1) did their best to undo the good work, opening the A's, then the X-through-Z's, then everything but the R's — the party at last turned up Rivet's personnel record: a peace-forged robot, hired as a quality control expert, terminated for "performance insufficiency" — a file suspiciously scrubbed of any detail about what, precisely, she'd been asking. The complaints in the files came from department heads — **Pel Firestone**, **Maxwell Truffle**, **Rusty Power Boost** — but the top-level sign-off, Rivet's included, belonged to **Henrietta Oll**. [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet), combing the nearest fifty files for a pattern (Investigation 23), found one: hire and termination dates sat almost exactly six months apart, and Henrietta's name was on every termination letter.

Before that, while the others wrestled the cabinets, [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet) had found something stranger still on the office whiteboard, which on a high Investigation check (26) she identified not as Toyota brainstorming at all, but as Ford marketing copy — luxury appointments, leather interiors, safety ratings, audio packages — smuggled wholesale into a rival company's internal notes. Someone inside Toyota Works was feeding the competitor's talking points straight into the walls.

Then footsteps, and the lights: HR head **Madison Ali** walked in on three strangers in her filing cabinets. The party's group Intimidation (26, 18, and a natural 1 from Scarlet, averaging a middling 15 until Silas burned a psychic die to lift it to 23) left her apologizing for not being in the office to receive corporate. She confirmed that Henrietta signs off on all hiring and termination, pointed them to Head Inspector **Pel Firestone** on the factory floor — Rivet's position, the last quality-control post, has never been backfilled — and, at Scarlet's insistence that Rivet be rehired precisely *because* her quality was so low, promised to make calls and get her back.

> "I'm Madison Ali. I'm head of HR. This is my office." — Madison Ali

### The Janitor in the Break Room

It was in the break room — half its fluorescent tubes dead, half its machines out of order, its snacks expired but preserved past all harm — that the vignette's true shape revealed itself. [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet) sniffed for goblin on a natural 20 (26) and learned only that the whole room smelled vile; [Silas](/player-characters/silas), calling for Bru over his psychic link (Perception 25), clocked the janitor mopping the same corner over and over. Beneath a hat of disguise, it was [Bru](/player-characters/bru) himself. His warning arrived psychically and immediately:

> "There's a camera above me... you're going to be found out pretty quickly."

Pressed for answers, Bru admitted — with the particular defensiveness of a goblin who has been caught — to a fire that had broken out on the factory floor:

> "Nobody saw me do it. You can't prove anything."

He went further: Rivet had been onto something real. Rivet was convinced Ford was behind the rot eating Toyota Works from the inside, and Bru — while granting that Ford makes a great engine — agreed it was "obviously Ford," though he could not prove it. Worse, he was fairly sure "old-school Iron Claws" were employed in the building. Whether this is a true echo of the gang that once ruled High Forge's underworld, or merely a vignette-world coincidence of naming, the Archive declines to settle; some mysteries are better left bottled, like a good vintage. Bru's lead, he revealed, came from none other than **Mr. Toyota** himself, the company's original owner, who wants it back and had told him that plants inside were dragging it down. Bru's own disguise was no longer any use — some high-level magic had seen through every change of it — and being seen with him would make the party accomplices.

Bru slipped behind a loose panel in the corner and asked them to follow without the camera seeing. [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet) attempted a cartwheel out of frame and landed it about as gracefully as a dropped filing cabinet (7), the camera following her the whole way down. [Leliana](/player-characters/leliana) managed the same maneuver with considerably more poise (15). [Silas](/player-characters/silas), never one to cartwheel when he could simply vanish, slipped through on a 23 — and with the camera fixed on Scarlet's escalating gymnastics, the other two reached the enclave unseen. Behind the panel: Rivet's hidden hideout, its walls scrawled with manifesto in a shaking hand — *"The reliability is a lie." "Ford spreads lies."*

> "You understand why I f***ing hate our guts at this point?" — Bru

### A Cipher in Sentence, Word, Letter

Before pushing them back out, Bru handed over the one thing he'd found and could not crack: a note of Rivet's, rows of numbers in clusters of three, with a line scrawled beneath in her hand — *they wrote it themselves; sentence, word, letter* — signed simply "R." [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet), who had never made it into the enclave, capped the break-room sequence with a floor routine on a natural 20, tumbling out of the break room and sticking the landing just outside the door while the camera swung uselessly after her. [Silas](/player-characters/silas) handed her the note. Her Intelligence check (18) was neither good nor bad, but enough to see the structure: each line a word, each cluster of three numbers a letter. It was, the table soon agreed, a book cipher — useless without the specific text it was keyed to — and Silas wondered aloud whether Toyota had an employee handbook it wrote itself. With the hour nearing eleven, the DM called the session on the cliffhanger, to be continued the following Friday: a cipher unsolved, an inspector unproven, and a goblin still hiding behind a break-room panel.

## Notable Character Moments

- **[Leliana](/player-characters/leliana)** had the sharpest eyes at the door (Perception 22), spotting the receptionist Scarlet was sure did not exist, and kept the B team's honor intact with a clean 15 on the cartwheel past the camera.
- **[Silas](/player-characters/silas)** anchored the infiltration — the Sleight of Hand 31 that tore through HR's filing cabinets, the psychic die that turned a middling Intimidation into a 23, the Perception 25 that unmasked the janitor, and the 23 that got him behind Rivet's panel without the camera catching him.
- **[Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet)** swung wildly between brilliance and farce: a 24 on the parking lot, a 26 cracking the Ford-in-Toyota's-clothing whiteboard, a natural 20 sniffing out nothing but a vile-smelling break room, and a natural 20 sticking her exit landing — bracketed by a natural 1 at the front door and a humiliating 7 on a cartwheel minutes earlier.
- **[Bru](/player-characters/bru)**, run this week by his own player from the far side of the DM's screen, got to be fully, gloriously unrepentant about the fire he started, refusing to cop to it even under direct questioning from his own friends.

## Themes

Corporate conspiracy as comedy, the thin disguise over a company eating itself alive, and the particular joy of a "filler" episode that still manages to matter — Bru alone, hunted, trusting no one but the people who always come when called. The vignette leaned hard into automotive satire (a Toyota factory secretly run on Ford's talking points, a city convinced that truck ownership is a character flaw) and into the campaign's long-running love of disguises: cover identities, hats of disguise, camera-dodging acrobatics, all stacked atop one another in a single break room.

## Session MVP

**Scarlet**, for carrying the night on rolls as mismatched as her cover identity demanded — a 24, a 26, and two natural 20s, with a natural 1 and an ignominious 7 thrown in to keep her honest. Honorable mention to Bru, for holding the entire vignette together from inside a janitor's disguise while denying arson to his own rescue party.
