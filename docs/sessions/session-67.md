## Plot Events

### A Hologram Named Help

The table's ongoing war against the Eldoran Empire paused this week for what this correspondent can only describe as a *palate cleanser* — a small-batch, hand-pulled vignette, distinct in terroir from the grand continental sweep of recent sessions. It opened with **Kevin**, the party's ever-reliable blur of a companion, arriving at speed bearing a flickering, static-choked holographic recording. The projection resolved into [Bru](/player-characters/bru), haggard, hunted, and unmistakably dramatic for the occasion:

> "Help me, Silas. You're my only hope."

Bru's message explained, in the clipped fashion of a man speaking quickly before his pursuers caught up, that he had gone to ground after an investigation into a "Tacoma" explosion had blown up — quite literally — in his face. He had hidden what he'd found inside the **Toyota Works** factory, in the worst part of town, and he needed the party to retrieve it. He added one more thread for them to pull: a night-shift inspector named **Rivet** had been fired recently, for the crime of asking questions about the very same Tacoma situation.

### Into the Ruins of Toyota Works

The factory itself was a study in false fronts — a gleaming white glass atrium stitched awkwardly onto a rotting industrial hulk, its workers visibly miserable and smoking exclusively outdoors, as if the building itself discouraged joy from entering. [Leliana](/player-characters/leliana) took the first pass at scouting, while [Silas](/player-characters/silas) examined the building's directory (Investigation 16) and found it a dreary roll call of corporate departments, broken only by a conspicuously placed "Marketing" office at Room 190. [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet), meanwhile, swept the parking lot (Perception 24) and flagged two oddities worth a chronicler's italics: a tarped-over car tucked into a side alley, and the complete absence of any actual Tacomas in the lot of a factory that manufactured nothing else — a detail made stranger by the world's long-running and never-quite-explained conviction that Tacoma owners are, as a class, perverts.

Disguises went on. [Silas](/player-characters/silas) became "Kid Sour Patch." [Leliana](/player-characters/leliana) became "Miss Airhead." [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet) became "Dr. Sour Straw." A fourth operative — the Archive's surviving notes grow regrettably threadbare here, and I decline to invent what was not recorded — went by "Dr. Warhead." Posing as corporate investigators looking into Rivet's dismissal, the quartet approached the front desk, where a receptionist waved them off without ceremony:

> "That old Clanka, she got fired weeks ago, honey."

Directed to Human Resources and finding the door locked, the party did what Taco Cat does best in the absence of a better plan: they kicked it down. The office was empty.

> *I am obligated to record that the receptionist's choice of slur for a peace-forged inspector went entirely unremarked upon by the party, who had larger doors to kick down. Some threads, alas, the Archive must leave dangling. — P.A.*

### Paper Trails and Pattern Firings

Rifling through a filing system clearly never organized by anyone who respected filing, the party turned up Rivet's personnel record: a peace-forged robot, hired as a quality control expert, terminated for "performance insufficiency" — a file suspiciously scrubbed of any detail about what, precisely, she'd been asking. A pattern emerged quickly: employees cycled out of Toyota Works roughly every six months, every termination letter signed by the same hand — **Henrietta Oll**.

[Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet) found something stranger still on a break-room whiteboard, which on a high Investigation check (26) she identified not as Toyota brainstorming at all, but as Ford marketing copy — luxury appointments, leather interiors, safety ratings, audio packages — smuggled wholesale into a rival company's internal notes. Someone inside Toyota Works was feeding the competitor's talking points straight into the walls.

Cornering HR head **Madison Ali** in her office produced a tidy, if unglamorous, Intimidation check (around 15) that pried loose two more names worth chasing — **Henrietta Oll**, who signs every termination, and **Pel Firestone**, Head Inspector of Quality Control — along with Madison's reluctant promise to make calls and get Rivet reinstated.

> "I'm Madison Ali. I'm head of HR. This is my office." — Madison Ali

### The Janitor in the Break Room

It was in the break room that the vignette's true shape revealed itself: a janitor sweeping quietly in the corner was, beneath an only partially effective hat of disguise, [Bru](/player-characters/bru) himself. His warning arrived psychically and immediately:

> "There's a camera above me... you're going to be found out pretty quickly."

Pressed for answers, Bru admitted — with the particular defensiveness of a goblin who has been caught — to a fire that had broken out on the factory floor:

> "Nobody saw me do it. You can't prove anything."

He went further: Rivet had been onto something real. Ford, he claimed, was behind the rot eating Toyota Works from the inside, with "old-school Iron Claws" muscle employed to keep quality — and morale — in the gutter. Whether this is a true echo of the gang that once ruled High Forge's underworld, or merely a vignette-world coincidence of naming, the Archive declines to settle; some mysteries are better left bottled, like a good vintage. Bru's employer, he revealed, was none other than **Mr. Toyota** himself, desperate to reclaim a company being sabotaged from within.

Avoiding the camera required a little theater. [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet) attempted a cartwheel out of frame and landed it about as gracefully as a dropped filing cabinet (7). [Leliana](/player-characters/leliana) managed the same maneuver with considerably more poise (15). [Silas](/player-characters/silas), never one to cartwheel when he could simply vanish, slipped behind a loose wall panel with Stealth 23. Behind it: Rivet's hidden hideout, its walls scrawled with manifesto in a shaking hand — *"The reliability is a lie." "Ford spreads lies."*

> "You understand why I f***ing hate our guts at this point?" — Bru

### A Cipher in Sentence, Word, Letter

[Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet) capped the break-room sequence with a natural 20, somersaulting cleanly out of the hideout and sticking the landing just outside the door like she'd rehearsed it. Tucked among Rivet's writings, the party found a coded note, signed simply with an "R," its message rendered not in words but in a string of numbers reading *sentence, word, letter*. It was, the table quickly agreed, a book cipher — useless without the specific text it was keyed to. Speculation turned, inevitably, to the Toyota employee handbook sitting somewhere in that building, possibly even one the party's own disguised personas were nominally supposed to have written. [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet)'s attempt to simply think her way through it (18) came up short, and with the hour nearing eleven, the DM called the session on the cliffhanger: a cipher unsolved, an inspector unproven, and a goblin still hiding in a break room closet.

## Notable Character Moments

- **[Leliana](/player-characters/leliana)** spent the session's opening half trying, three separate times, to reach Bru by *Message* spell before the party ever set foot in the factory — each attempt failing against whatever was jamming his signal, a small, stubborn thread of worry running under the comedy.
- **[Silas](/player-characters/silas)** anchored the infiltration with its two cleanest rolls of the night — the placard read (16) that mapped the building, and the Stealth 23 that got him behind Rivet's panel without a flicker of the camera catching him.
- **[Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet)** swung wildly between brilliance and farce: a 24 on the parking lot, a 26 cracking the Ford-in-Toyota's-clothing whiteboard, a natural 20 sticking her exit landing — bracketed by a humiliating 7 attempting the exact same cartwheel minutes earlier.
- **[Bru](/player-characters/bru)**, run this week by his own player from the far side of the DM's screen, got to be fully, gloriously unrepentant about the fire he started, refusing to cop to it even under direct questioning from his own friends.

## Themes

Corporate conspiracy as comedy, the thin disguise over a company eating itself alive, and the particular joy of a "filler" episode that still manages to matter — Bru alone, hunted, trusting no one but the people who always come when called. The vignette leaned hard into automotive satire (a Toyota factory secretly run on Ford's talking points, a city convinced that truck ownership is a character flaw) and into the campaign's long-running love of disguises: cover identities, hats of disguise, camera-dodging acrobatics, all stacked atop one another in a single break room.

## Session MVP

**Scarlet**, for carrying the night on rolls as mismatched as her cover identity demanded — a 24, a 26, and a natural 20, with a single ignominious 7 thrown in to keep her honest. Honorable mention to Bru, for holding the entire vignette together from inside a janitor's disguise while denying arson to his own rescue party.
