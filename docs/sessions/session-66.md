*Compiled by Vellum, Archival Construct of the Moonfall Chronicle*

## Players Present
Taylor Ramsey (Silas Fairbanks) and Justin Hale (Bru) were at the table this session. Luke Neverisky (Leliana Goldspring/Helisanna) and Ellis Taylor (Olivia Cooper) were away; the DM carried both characters through the swamp, the radio, and the schooner, and the [Pocket Village](/locations/pocket-village) capsule in the back of the stolen Jeep let the table quietly rotate whoever wasn't needed in a given scene out of the active narrative. The Archive notes, with the pointed serenity appropriate to a masthead recently and decisively settled, that recent irregularities in that masthead have no bearing on tonight's record.

## Plot Events

### A Scout Reconsidered
The session picked up mid-flight from the Goldspring farm, the party still running on the stolen Jeep Silas hotwired at the end of last session. Silas floated the idea of peeling off alone for a solo stealth approach to whatever lay ahead — a notion the rest of the group talked him out of before it could cost him anything. They stayed together, continuing north toward the coast to make the rendezvous [George](/npcs/george) and [Finnegan](/npcs/finnegan) had arranged by boat.

> "I really miss the days when we were just petty criminals who just got to mind their own business." — Silas Fairbanks

### The Truck in the Fungus
The road north ran through swampy, overgrown terrain gone badly wrong — a military truck sat half-swallowed by an aggressive fungal growth, strung together with a sticky yellow webbing that dissolved metal on contact and choked anyone unlucky enough to breathe its spores. A Nature check of 19 confirmed what the smell already suggested: this was not a natural swamp infection. Silas's Medicine check (21) read the truck's occupants as salvageable, and over Bru's pragmatic counter-proposal —

> "We might help by making their car lighter... or let them die and steal parts." — Bru

— the party chose to burn their way in rather than loot and leave. Bru's flamethrower cleared the webbing from the truck's frame scorch by scorch, buying the group a path through rather than a grave marker.

### The Humvee in the Swamp
Deeper in, a Humvee sat stuck axle-deep with its engine still idling and three soldiers aboard in varying stages of infection — boils, spores, the whole miserable catalogue. Two were too far gone to move; the party confirmed as much by poking them with a stick from a respectful distance and left them where they lay. The third, a tanned, tattooed, blonde Australian soldier of Unit 34 named Private Jeffers Walaby, was judged salvageable enough to be worth keeping nearby — and more importantly, worth impersonating.

### Private Walaby Goes On Air
With the real Walaby unconscious, Silas took his name and his radio, dropping into a broad Australian accent to field incoming military chatter without missing a beat.

> "G'day mate. Ran into a bit of a bit of a wing dinger here in the deep green... still no sign of the quarry." — Silas Fairbanks, as "Private Walaby"

The deception held at a staggering 32, past a superior officer who tried to interject —

> "He don't want to talk to you... he's telling me that I should be like flipping you off and cussing you out." — Silas Fairbanks

— and through to command's unsuspecting sign-off ("All right, Private Walaby. Well, good work getting out of there..."). More than cover, it was intelligence: the radio confirmed the Empire's forces are actively hunting the party by name for the attack on the capital ([Session 63](/sessions/session-63), [Session 64](/sessions/session-64)), and that two units have gone dark — one working door-to-door through the farming country the party had just fled, which command kept calling "the Shire," and another somewhere in "the deep green" — this swamp, not ten minutes before, reporting aggressive fauna and begging for help that never came.

### Walaby's Bad Morning
The real Walaby chose the worst possible moment to regain consciousness, groggy and furious to find himself abandoned mid-swamp.

> "What? You got to strand me out here? What happened to Tuni and Starlight?" — Private Walaby

— a question that went unanswered, not least because the party had no idea who Tuni and Starlight were, and even less interest in finding out. When Walaby tried to stand despite every warning about the spores, Bru answered with a *Shatter*. Walaby's Constitution save came up a natural 1 — a final total of 5 against DC 16 — and fourteen points of thunder damage put him back down for good, headache and all. He stayed behind with the other two. The Humvee did not.

> *The Archive notes, for the record, that "Tuni" is also the name of a coworker Mr. Bru's associates met inside the Eldoran spaceport factory in [Session 63](/sessions/session-63). Whether this is coincidence, recurrence, or a filing error several worlds deep, the Archive declines to resolve. Some threads are left loose on purpose. — V.*

### Hats and a Humvee
With Walaby's identity already spent on the radio, the party finished the job properly: Hats of Disguise turned Silas and Bru into passable Eldoran soldiers, and the commandeered Humvee — minigun stripped for good measure — carried them the rest of the way toward the coast, Silas burning a short rest and his hit dice along the way to patch up the damage still lingering from the angel fight over the capital ([Session 64](/sessions/session-64)).

### George's Warning and a Stolen Schooner
A burst of panic over the [sending stone](/items/sending-stones) came through as the coast came into view: George, warning of chaos at the harbor and a feeling of being watched.

> "There's chaos here at the harbor. I'm a little worried that we're being tracked... don't look like yourselves." — George

The party reached the pier to find Finnegan had made good on the boat — a schooner, stolen roughly thirty minutes prior, crewed now by George, his boyfriend Dylan (the two had met at the [Sexy Peacock](/locations/sexy-peacock) back in [Session 58](/sessions/session-58)), Finnegan himself, and a handful of children along for the ride. Finnegan's distaste for engines showed in the boat's speed —

> "It's also slow as shit. What the fuck is wrong with you?" — Silas Fairbanks
> "I stole this boat like 30 minutes ago." — Finnegan

— but there was no time to shop around. Bru wired the Humvee with timed charges, drove it off the end of the pier himself, and let the water and the explosives finish the job before anyone could trace it back to a trio of Unit 34 soldiers who were never coming home.

### The Thing in the Water
A submersible was spotted roughly a mile out, closing fast enough to reach engagement range inside fifteen minutes. Bru's passive Arcana (13) caught the first wrong detail, and a dropped signal sparker charge — sixty feet of sudden light — confirmed the rest on an Insight check that climbed from 14 to 18 on a reroll: this was no submarine. It was a building-sized humanoid war machine, four points of glowing purple-white crystal lit at the shoulders and hips, and a red, spinning sensor node mounted above one shoulder blade like an antenna.

Bru climbed into the schooner's bird's nest for a cleaner shot and put a tier-two concussive grenade square into that node — thunder damage, a knockdown if it landed — only for the recoil to pitch him straight off the rigging. He and the grenade hit their marks at the same instant, Bru taking a fall's worth of lightning damage on the way down, saved from the sea itself only by Silas, who swung a sail out like a ramp and caught him clean on a natural 20 dexterity save.

The machine didn't correct course. The schooner cut hard to starboard; the thing in the water kept walking its original line and didn't follow. The party watched it recede, unchallenged and unexplained, and turned the schooner's bow out toward the North Sea.

## Notable Character Moments
- **Silas Fairbanks** carried the session's riskiest con start to finish — a stolen identity, a 32 Deception check, and an Australian accent good enough to talk a dying unit's command off the search — then followed it up by catching Bru clean out of the air on a natural 20.
- **Bru** ran the session's demolitions arc beginning to end: flamethrower through the fungus, timed charges on the Humvee, a signal flare and a bird's-nest grenade shot against something the size of a building, and a fall he very nearly didn't walk away from.
- The quiet, grim efficiency of leaving three soldiers — one of them twice, having first used his name and then his silence — behind in a swamp said more about how far the party has had to harden than any line of dialogue could.

## Themes
Survival arithmetic ran the whole session: help enough to keep a conscience, not so much that it costs the mission. Identity theft doubled as both weapon and shield — Walaby's name bought safety on the radio and then earned him a Shatter to the skull for trying to stand up and reclaim it. And the swamp's dissolving fungus, the Empire's silent hunt, and a war machine the size of a building all point the same direction: whatever the party broke open in the capital, it is still looking for them, and it does not plan to stop.

## Session MVP
**Silas Fairbanks.** A 32 on the Deception check that bought the party a clean radio exit, followed by the natural 20 that put him under Bru in freefall with a sail instead of open water — nobody else touched two separate saves-the-day rolls in one session.
