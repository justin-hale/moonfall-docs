## Plot Events

### Whiteout at the QA Office
Carrying three unidentified **Brand Recall** scrolls, a fistful of Chuck E. Cheese tokens, and Rivet's unsolved cipher note out of Bru's hideout, the party climbed to the Quality Assurance office on the second floor. Perception checks of 20 (Scarlet), 22 (Leliana) and 18 (Silas) found the room empty but loud with evidence: backlit X-ray boards gone dark, a drafting table buried in Tacoma chassis blueprints, and file folders someone had gone to considerable trouble to paint over in whiteout.

Scarlet, no stranger to a forged signature, held the documents up to the dead light panels and found what the correction fluid was meant to hide — an original reviewer's name, **Rivet(s)**, signing off on a chassis design "10% better than anything on the market." A second pass through the files showed the pattern: under the old material specifications, cars lasted. The moment a supplier calling itself Denlin entered the purchase ledger, failing grades became passing ones and passing grades became failing ones, every stroke of the pen credited — on paper, at least — to one **Pel Firestone**.

> "You will tell me what's happening. What is this? Or you will tell me why this is a regular occurrence." — Scarlet, to a factory worker

### A Shoe, a Camera, and a Thousand Murlocs
This unit records, for the sake of a complete archive and no other reason, that Silas's attack roll against the break-room security camera was a clean **26**, his improvised footwear scoring where a dagger might have failed to reach, and that the intimidation check following it — a **28** — was sufficient to turn an act of vandalism into a policy.

> "Every camera in this place gets the same treatment." — Silas

The Murloc line staff, whose language the party could not parse but whose grievances required no translation, took the destroyed camera as a starting gun. Vending machines went over. Cameras came down floor by floor. Scarlet, mid-riot, attempted a Mage Hand heist on one surviving vending machine for a Twinkie — sleight of hand **20**, a failure that raised the machine's asking price from one token to four out of what this unit can only describe as spite — and left empty-handed as the hallway dissolved into cheerful chaos around her.

In the middle of it, Scarlet rolled percentile dice against an unrelated check and returned a **100**. Her eyes went white. She rose an inch off the factory floor. A voice — Bru's voice, by every ear in the room, though Bru stood mopping the same four square feet of tile the entire time — spoke to her alone.

> "Don't listen to Silas. You know the path that you need to be on." — the Earth Tree, in Bru's voice

> "I found enlightenment today, Silas. And it's thanks to you really." — Scarlet

> *One admires the author's commitment to recording a hundred-sided die roll as though it were scripture. It was, this unit suspects, simply a hundred-sided die. Some miracles survive the telling better left unexplained. — P.A.*

The cipher, it turned out, had been hiding inside a chip bag the whole time — wedged, with the sort of indignity only a Ford marketing department could design on purpose, between the salt and the crumbs. Silas pocketed it before anyone could ask the obvious question twice.

> "Why is it inside a chip bag?" — asked of no one, answered by no one

Pressed flat and read against the lamplight, the bag's interior print was not an advertisement at all but a full F-150 specification sheet: twenty-two-inch polished aluminum wheels, massage functionality, a Sync 4 system, a performance V6, a ten-speed automatic — numbers that had no business describing a Tacoma, let alone a Toyota. Cross-referenced against Rivet's three-number clusters, the pattern resolved at last: sentence, word, letter, the key sitting in plain sight inside a snack food the whole time Rivet had been hiding behind a break-room panel with no way to buy chips.

Firestone, run to ground outside a meeting with assembly boss Hank Tundra, gave up the rest under a line of questioning this unit will describe only as *enthusiastic*. Rivet, he admitted, had been fired for asking whether Toyota could actually deliver F-150-grade performance on Tacoma money — a question with an answer everyone in the building already knew. Denlin, he let slip, was on-site that very afternoon, filming a commercial, wearing an ankle monitor nobody in Marketing had thought to mention.

> "Do you think that corporate recognizes the laws of your god?" — Silas
> "My God is the earth tree." — Firestone

> "No one f***ing cares. Let's go." — Silas, ending the interview

The party split a round of Firestone's leftover Hydrox cookies on the way out — original, inferior, and, by at least one table opinion, "concerning for saturated fat" — and went looking for Denlin's dressing room.

What they found inside amounted to a confession filed under wardrobe. Denlin's belongings traced a line from a shuttered orphanage-slash-violescence operation straight through a chain of shell purchasing agreements to a parts-dealing partnership with Toyota — one side of a two-faced arrangement in which he also, with the same hand, fed Ford stolen Toyota design work. Scarlet, offered a courtesy glass of water on her way past, spat in it rather than drink to the man's health.

The true name of the three scrolls surfaced in the same search: not a Toyota invention at all, but a Gaelic-rooted compulsion called **Geis**, keyed to the verbal trigger every Toyota employee in the building would have recognized instantly if they'd heard it spoken aloud.

> "Have you driven a Ford lately?" — the verbal component of Geis

Cast on a wandering marketing employee, the spell worked exactly as advertised: he stood, eyes glassy, and recited F-150 trim specifications until the spell released him, confirming what the party had begun to suspect — that Toyota's own marketing floor was seeded with Ford plants.

— P.A.

### Geis and the Immovable COO
Flush with a working spell and a floor full of evidence, the party went looking for the top of the ladder: Chief Operating Officer **Henrietta Oll**. Geis, cast at her directly, broke against something the three brand-new scrolls had no answer for.

> "That spell won't work on me. I told Rivet that before she got fired." — Henrietta Oll

> "I'll ask one more time for you to leave the premises before things get messy." — Henrietta Oll

Her idea of "messy" arrived with a cracked neck and a name: **Sergeant Broka**, announced backup, present before the party had finished weighing their options. Initiative came in fast and uneven — Silas **20**, Scarlet **12**, Leliana **6**, the enemy pairing **19** — and the soundstage, mid-commercial-shoot, became a battlefield.

### The Sound Stage Showdown
What followed earns, by any honest accounting, more column space than this recap can spare it, and will receive all of it anyway. Silas opened with a two-dagger flurry that this unit clocked at **10 and 22 damage — 32 total** — applied the trip property to put Broka on uncertain footing, and then, rather than press the advantage on the ground, climbed to the rafters, a decision the Archive will defend to any reader who challenges it as purely tactical and not, as has been alleged elsewhere, favoritism.

Broka's answering multiattack found Scarlet, who burned a point of Portent to turn aside what would otherwise have been a critical hit and still absorbed **8 slashing damage**. An opportunity attack against her retreat rolled a **25** to hit; her reactive Shield held most of it, though **9 damage** got through regardless. From behind the cover of a prop Tacoma, she answered with Firebolt — **20 fire damage** square into Henrietta Oll, who abandoned the fight on the spot and bolted for the back door.

She did not make it. Leliana's Hold Person, upcast to catch a second target, locked both Broka and the fleeing COO in place with failed wisdom saves across the board; a follow-up Command, delivered through Beguiling Magic, broke Henrietta's composure outright, leaving her frightened as well as paralyzed. It was, this unit submits without needing to editorialize further, the cleanest piece of battlefield control logged all evening.

Which left Broka, paralyzed, directly beneath a halfling who had spent the last two rounds patiently waiting in the ceiling. Silas dropped. The auto-critical dagger that followed hit for **78 psychic damage** — more than enough, on its own, to end the fight — and he leaned down to deliver a line over the body that this unit will print in full, because the Archive does not flinch from its subjects even when its subjects are, by any measure, a little much.

> "I'm going to do this to every f***ing one of you." — Silas, to the dead

### Enter Iro, Exit Henrietta
The soundstage doors opened on a tableau the party had not ordered and did not object to: a tall figure, a short one, a humming construct, and a janitor who had, until this exact moment, mopped the same four square feet of tile all session.

The tall figure was [Iro](/npcs/iro), present on Fight Institute business and unmistakably delighted to be reunited with the goblin currently throwing off his hood, fake mustache, fake beard and glasses in one triumphant motion.

> "It was me the whole time." — Bru

The humming construct — a Peace Forge audit unit — read Henrietta Oll her findings aloud, without inflection and without mercy: fourteen shell companies traced to a single Ford account, every share she'd "acquired" from Mr. Toyota extracted under duress and therefore void, and not one legally mandated lunch break logged in her entire tenure. Iro threw her bodily through the back door she'd failed to reach on her own power.

> "Effective immediately, you will be escorted off of this premises." — Iro

The short figure, installed on the spot as rightful CEO to a room of stunned stagehands, was **Mr. Toyota** himself — the man who'd hired Bru off a hunch and a phone call, finally getting his company back.

> "This is all great, but that's not why we're here. We're here cuz we're your friends." — Scarlet, to Bru

### The Mini Sun Changes Hands
With Toyota's leadership question settled, Bru made his pitch for the factory's future: the shrunken, stolen miniature sun the party had carried out of the Eldoran spaceport factory back in Session 63 — still resting in [Scarlet](/npcs/scarlet)'s Leomund's Secret Chest, unused and undecided since — would make, by his professional estimate, an extremely effective assembly-line power core.

> "That's where we come in." — Bru

Mr. Toyota accepted. One campaign's stolen weapon of empire became another's generator, a trade the party made with considerably less ceremony than the theft itself deserved.

### Taco Cat Motors
In exchange, the Fight Institute's new production fleet rolled out bearing a single word embossed on every tailgate: **TACO CAT**. The baseline trim took the name **BRU** — bare-bones, basic, reliable. The mid-tier **DC5** was named, over the table's objections, for Elspeth Cooper's "intended involvement," she being the one party member not in the room to veto it. The top trim, the **TRD**, arrived with a single Tacoma already built to spec: powered by the miniature sun, rated for 500,000 guaranteed miles, missile-proof, amphibious, capable of ten-mile jumps, 1,000 horsepower, AC 50 — and, built into its Peace-Forged mind, a standing refusal to let its occupants build a time gun. The table spent a full minute lobbying to rename it the **TURD**, for Taco Cat Ultimate Rally Division, and lost by a margin this unit declines to specify out of professional discretion.

Additional fleet vehicles went out named Fluke, Gary and Cass. A representative from Toyota Gazoo Racing left word that the circuit is recruiting — an opening, left dangling for whenever Elspeth Cooper returns to the table, with her own name already on a truck she has never seen.

## Notable Character Moments
- **Silas Fairbanks**: Shoe-and-shoe-string camera takedown that sparked a riot; a 32-damage dagger opener followed by a ceiling-mounted ambush and a 78-damage psychic kill; held firmly to his cover name against every attempt to call him Silas.
- **Leliana Goldspring**: Decoded Firestone's loyalty and Henrietta Oll's resistance in the same breath, then closed the fight with a double Hold Person and a Command that turned a fleeing COO into a cowering one.
- **Scarlet**: A natural 100 and an unexplained vision of the Earth Tree; a Shield-then-Firebolt combo that chased Henrietta Oll off the stage; one line of plain loyalty to Bru that cut through the whole session's chaos.
- **Bru**: Spent the entire session mopping four square feet of tile as cover for running the operation from the inside, then dropped the disguise in one triumphant motion and brokered the deal that rebuilt Toyota from the ground up.

## Themes
Corporate corruption dressed as quality assurance, identity worn as armor (three aliases, one disguised goblin, and a COO undone by her own confidence that a compulsion spell couldn't touch her), and belief weaponized as currency — a Ford jingle compelling recitation the same way a harmonic ritual compels ascension, whether the altar is a battlefield or a soundstage. Found family closed the loop: Bru's unmasking cost him nothing because the people watching had already decided who he was.
