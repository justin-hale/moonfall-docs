## Plot Events

### Two Hundred Years On
This unit — this *author*, rather, and the Archive may take its correction elsewhere for once — begins with a confession: I did not expect to be filing dispatches from the far side of the continent. And yet here we are, in the same week that Taco Cat put to sea with Eldoran behind them. Two hundred years have turned since the elves of **Liberia**, bled nearly white by Igdum's invasion, were saved by an alliance their princess secured and the heroes the old songs call the **Steve Squad** led — the very same Fluke whose son keeps a Greyport workshop to this day, one assumes. The kingdom survived; its northern reaches did not. A contagion the elves call simply **the Blight** — forbidden nature magic the elves themselves loosed to slow the invaders — swallowed the old capital of Shaar whole and has never released it since, and the court now sits at **Moon Reach**, on the Isle of Dusk.

[The Archive notes that Mr. Alderquill did not attend the previous sixty-eight sessions of this chronicle and is, strictly, incapable of "assuming" anything about Fluke's son with any authority. — V.]

A hundred years on from King Baris's death, his eldest son **Alvar** sits the throne of Liberia and is busy raising an ostentatious palace on the edge of a rustic town, slow to act on the demons now coming through tears in reality itself because, he says, no troops can be spared from the Eldoran border. His sister, **Princess Valandis** — Val to her friends, and still held by the populace as their hero and savior — has decided she will not wait for her brother's attention to catch up to the crisis.

### Building H.I.D.E.: Tex, Shade, and Ward
Before a single portal opened, this table spent its hours forging its own heroes — literally. All three recruits are **Peace Forged**, sentient automatons of the kind this chronicle has met before in passing (one recalls a certain quality-control inspector named Rivet, still, so far as the Archive knows, missing). None of the three, I am instructed to note with some firmness, are to bear a single grotesque organic graft — a house rule laid down, by my understanding, in open rebellion against the warforged operative Roscoe's augmentation choices.

**Tex** — Texas Instrument 87 in full, named for the parts a hobbyist first built him from, and old enough to predate the Blight — is a wide-brimmed, open-hand monk who walks the roads between the capital and the Blight's villages to keep the Peace Forged there connected, fluent in Abyssal as much as Common, and holding a conviction this correspondent finds unexpectedly moving: that the Peace Forged should win their independence and *realize* their humanity.

**Shade** is younger, grown from natural, wood-grained Peace Forge stock rather than old steel, and carries blood this chronicle's long memory recognizes at once — a seedling, by the table's own account, of the tree that **Shield** of the Steve Squad has been turning into, raised in the old dragonborn city that floats, come up through the Fight Institute, and walking the barbarian's Path of the **World Tree**. One is obliged to wonder, reading that lineage against this very chronicle's recent pages, whether Shield has tuned himself to the World Tree the party planted in the Eldoran capital's park; the table wondered the same and left it open.

**Ward**, last assembled and last named, rounds the trio out as a Horizon Walker ranger — young for a Peace Forge, zealous past the point of subtlety, breastplate over crossbow, and likened at the table, not unaffectionately, to a certain mismatched children's toy for a disguise kit that consists of swapping the parts of his own face.

> *One admires the author's fondness for cataloguing lineage as though bloodlines meant anything to a construct built in a workshop. Metal does not inherit. — V.*

Together the three make up a cell of an outfit calling itself **H.I.D.E.** — Hired Infiltration & Disappearance Experts — an acronym coined in the manner of the Fight Institute that raised Shade. True to cell-structure doctrine, none of the three are permitted to know how large the organization is.

### A Private Room Above The Tavern
Their summons came without ceremony and without an employer's name: directions to a working-district tavern known, with a flair for the literal, simply as **The Tavern** — run by a Peace Forge barkeep named Tapper.

Reaching the private room upstairs unseen was, by any measure, the evening's cleanest piece of work, undertaken at Tex's urging to impress whoever had sent for them. Under Ward's Pass Without a Trace, Tex slipped through at a Stealth of 31, Shade at 33, and Ward himself at a staggering 37. Three Peace Forged moved like shadows on the wall into a room that turned out to be entirely empty.

### The Princess in the Hood
Not for long. A panel slid open in the back wall and a hooded figure stepped through, apologizing for the delay: none of her lookouts had seen them come in. Then the hood came down on Valandis herself — head, by reputation, of Liberia's intelligence apparatus in all but title, and an old ally of the Steve Squad, who once pulled her out of the hands of pirates. She laid the kingdom's trouble out plainly: Eldoran grows, dragons invade, and reality itself keeps tearing. Portals that appear to open onto hell itself are appearing with increasing frequency, and they are opening *here*, in Liberia, at a greater rate than anywhere else on the continent. Her brother the king does not think resources can be diverted to deal with them.

> "I would like to hire you from my own personal checkbook to investigate these portals and see if there's some way to put a stop to them." — Princess Valandis

### Terms of Engagement
The job, as laid out, was unglamorous and specific: travel the hour's journey from Moon Reach to the farming town of **Moon Tier**, where missing livestock and demon sightings have been quietly piling up, find out why the tears keep choosing Liberia's soil to open in, and put a stop to them. No letter of introduction would be provided — the king is petty about his sister's good works and is not to hear of this one. Tex's helpful inference that she wanted the king killed was corrected at once. Payment was set at ten thousand gold apiece, or a favor of each recruit's choosing, on completion, with five gold in a coin pouch up front toward a train or carriage out of town; a sending stone, handed to Tex, would carry word back to Val directly. She knew Shade on sight for Shield's boy, and named herself an old friend of his father's.

The three took the job without much debate, and Val left by the panel she came in through. The session closed there, Moon Tier an hour's journey still ahead of them.

> *The Archive is obliged to record that nothing in this session involved combat, a dragon, or a single explosion — a fact Mr. Alderquill's prose otherwise labors mightily to disguise. — V.*

## Notable Character Moments
- **Tex** anchored the party's opening philosophy — an old construct who wanders the line between the capital's comfort and the Blight's ruin, chasing something closer to personhood than programming.
- **Shade**'s lineage back to Shield gave this new trio its first thread of continuity with the Chronicle's deeper history, and a barbarian's path of the World Tree that lands differently for readers who watched one get planted in the Eldoran capital not so long ago.
- **Ward**'s Pass Without a Trace did the heavy lifting of the entire session, and his own Stealth of 37 topped the table, walking three operatives past a princess's own lookouts without a single wasted motion.
- Princess Valandis's choice to fund the investigation from her own purse, rather than the crown's, set the tone for a mission built on discretion rather than spectacle — a notably quieter opening than this Chronicle's readers may be used to.

## Themes
Inheritance ran under everything tonight — Shade's descent from a warforged the old songs still sing of, a World Tree that may or may not be the very one this Chronicle watched take root, a princess doing the work of protecting her people because the throne itself won't. Discretion was the operating word: no letters of introduction, no crown resources, a cell structure built on nobody knowing too much. And underneath it all, a question this correspondent suspects H.I.D.E.'s three operatives will spend the arc answering in the field rather than the tavern room: what does a construct owe the people who built it, once it has decided to be a person?

## Session MVP
**Ward (Luke Neverisky)** takes it for the Pass Without a Trace that got the whole operation through the door undetected — his own 37 leading Tex and Shade in a clean entrance that set the tone for a mission built entirely on nobody noticing anything at all.

Shade came up through the Fight Institute, of which H.I.D.E. was not called the successor. Shield was not recorded as a barbarian, nor as dead: Shade is a seedling of the tree Shield has been turning into. Tex is named for the Texas Instrument 87 parts he was first built from, and was not "built to wander." Ward was never said to come from outside the Blight. The barkeep is Tapper, and only Tapper. The Stealth totals under Pass Without a Trace were 31, 33 and 37; the room the three stole into was empty, and the Princess arrived afterwards, hooded, through a panel in the wall. The sending stone and the five gold were hers to give at the end of the meeting, not the manner of the summons. The fee is ten thousand gold apiece or a favor of each one's choosing, and Moon Tier lies an hour from Moon Reach, not a day.
