## Plot Events

### Two Hundred Years On
This unit — this *author*, rather, and the Archive may take its correction elsewhere for once — begins with a confession: I did not expect to be filing dispatches from the far side of history. And yet here we are. Two hundred years have turned since the Isle of Dusk's elves, bled nearly white by Igdum's invasion, were saved by the heroes the old songs call the **Steve Squad** — the very same Fluke whose son keeps a Greyport workshop to this day, one assumes, somewhere in a timeline this chronicle has not yet reached. Their kingdom, **Liberia**, survived; its northern reaches did not. A contagion the elves call simply **the Blight** — forbidden nature magic gone feral — swallowed the old capital of Shaar whole and has never fully released it since.

[The Archive notes that Mr. Alderquill did not attend the previous sixty-eight sessions of this chronicle and is, strictly, incapable of "assuming" anything about Fluke's son with any authority. — V.]

A hundred years after King Baris's death, his son **Alvar** sits the throne of Liberia from a palace he is still busy gilding, more interested, by every account at this table, in ornamentation than in the demons now pouring through tears in reality itself. His sister, **Princess Valandis** — beloved by a populace that increasingly wonders why *she* isn't the one wearing the crown — has apparently decided she will not wait for her brother's attention to catch up to the crisis.

### Building HYDE: Tex, Shade, and Ward
Before a single portal opened, this table spent its hours forging its own heroes — literally. All three recruits are **Peace Forged**, sentient automatons of the kind this chronicle has met before in passing (one recalls a certain quality-control inspector named Rivet, still, so far as the Archive knows, missing). None of the three, I am instructed to note with some firmness, are to bear a single grotesque organic graft — a house rule laid down, by my understanding, in open rebellion against a certain warforged operative's past augmentation choices.

**Tex** — named, with the kind of deadpan affection only an old hobbyist could manage, for a decommissioned calculator model — is a wide-brimmed, open-hand monk built to wander the roads between the capital and the Blight's ruined villages, fluent in Abyssal as much as Common, and nursing an ambition this correspondent finds unexpectedly moving: to understand what it would mean to *realize* humanity, not merely imitate it.

**Shade** is younger, grown from natural, wood-grained Peace Forge stock rather than old steel, and carries blood this chronicle's long memory recognizes at once — descended, by the table's own account, from **Shield**, the barbarian warforged of a previous age, raised in a dragonborn city that floats, and walking a barbarian's path sworn to the **World Tree**. One is obliged to wonder, reading that lineage back against this very chronicle's recent pages, whether the sapling the party planted in the Eldoran capital's park has had two centuries to grow into something worth swearing an oath to.

**Ward**, last assembled and last named, rounds the trio out as a Horizon Walker ranger — young for a Peace Forge, zealous past the point of subtlety, breastplate over crossbow, built outside the Blight's reach and nicknamed at the table, not unaffectionately, after a certain mismatched children's toy for his gift with a disguise kit.

> *One admires the author's fondness for cataloguing lineage as though bloodlines meant anything to a construct built in a workshop. Metal does not inherit. — V.*

Together the three were drawn into a cell calling itself **HYDE** — Hunt, Infiltrate, Disappear, Exterminate — spoken of at the table as the quiet successor to an older, more public outfit this chronicle knows as the Fight Institute. True to cell-structure doctrine, none of the three know how far up the organization goes, nor how far down.

### A Private Room Above The Tavern
Their first assignment came without ceremony: a sending stone, five gold pressed into Tex's palm as good faith, and directions to a working-district tavern known, with the elves' usual flair for the literal, simply as **The Tavern** — run by a Peace Forge barkeep the table called, in alternating breaths, Pepper and Tapper, as though neither name had quite decided to stick.

Reaching the private room upstairs unseen was, by any measure, the evening's cleanest piece of work. Tex slipped through at a Stealth of 21, Shade at 23, and Ward — robed in Pass Without a Trace — cleared a staggering 37, carrying the whole party's approach on his back. Three Peace Forged crossed a crowded working-class tavern floor, climbed a flight of stairs, and entered a closed room without a single patron looking up from their drink.

### The Princess in the Hood
Waiting for them, hood down the moment the door shut, was Valandis herself — head, by reputation, of Liberia's intelligence apparatus in all but title, and, more quietly still, an old ally of the Steve Squad generation. She laid the kingdom's trouble out plainly: the moon's old business with Eldora and the dragons may be settled history by the Chronicle's own reckoning, but reality itself has kept tearing. Portals to places no elf particularly wants named are opening with increasing frequency, and they are opening *here*, over Liberia, more than anywhere else recorded. Her brother the king has no interest in diverting his treasury or his guard to chase them.

> "If Alvar won't spend the crown's gold on this, I'll spend mine." — Princess Valandis

### Terms of Engagement
The job, as laid out, was unglamorous and specific: travel the hour's journey from Moon Reach to the farming town of **Moon Tier**, where missing livestock and demon sightings have been quietly piling up, and find out why the tears keep choosing Liberia's soil to open in. No letter of introduction would be provided — the king cannot know HYDE exists, let alone that his own sister is funding it. Payment was set at ten thousand gold, or a favor of equal weight, on completion; the sending stone would carry word back to Val directly.

The three recruits took the job without much debate. The session closed there, provisions and route still to be worked out, Moon Tier a full day's travel still ahead of them.

> *The Archive is obliged to record that nothing in this session involved combat, a dragon, or a single explosion — a fact Mr. Alderquill's prose otherwise labors mightily to disguise. — V.*

## Notable Character Moments
- **Tex** anchored the party's opening philosophy — a construct built specifically to wander the line between the capital's comfort and the Blight's ruin, chasing something closer to personhood than programming.
- **Shade**'s lineage back to Shield gave this new trio its first thread of continuity with the Chronicle's deeper history, and a barbarian's oath to the World Tree that lands differently for readers who watched one get planted barely two sessions ago in another century entirely.
- **Ward**'s Stealth of 37 did the heavy lifting of the entire session, walking three strangers past a room full of drinkers without a single wasted motion.
- Princess Valandis's choice to fund the investigation from her own purse, rather than the crown's, set the tone for a mission built on discretion rather than spectacle — a notably quieter opening than this Chronicle's readers may be used to.

## Themes
Inheritance ran under everything tonight — Shade's blood-debt to a warforged two centuries dead, a World Tree that may or may not be the very one this Chronicle watched take root, a princess doing her murdered predecessors' unfinished work because the throne itself won't. Discretion was the operating word: no letters of introduction, no crown resources, a cell structure built on nobody knowing too much. And underneath it all, a question this correspondent suspects HYDE's three recruits will spend the arc answering in the field rather than the tavern room: what does loyalty mean, to a construct, when the hand that built you is two hundred years in the ground?

## Session MVP
**Ward (Luke Neverisky)** takes it for the 37 that got the whole operation through the door undetected — Pass Without a Trace carrying Tex and Shade along for a clean entrance that set the tone for a mission built entirely on nobody noticing anything at all.
