Justin Hale (Bru), Ellis Taylor (Olivia Cooper), and Zack (Ohma Kapua) were absent; Bru and Olivia remained off-page, the one building bombs and the other asleep. ## Plot Events

### Splitting the Strike: Three Teams, One Signal
With the university tower, the spaceport factory, and the barracks all confirmed as live targets, the party sat down to lock the operation's shape. George — still embedded, still walking the line between guard duty and resistance work — weighed in on timing with characteristic bluntness:

> "Probably in the middle of the day. Because everyone's out on patrol. Unless you're trying to kill a bunch of people... then in the middle of the night." — George

Midday won out for the concert hall detonation, chosen explicitly to thin the casualty count. On that math, at least, the room spoke with one voice.

> "We're killing infrastructure." — Elspeth Cooper

George reminded the table of the obvious consequence waiting on the other side of ignition:

> "This city is going to go on lockdown as soon as the explosion goes off." — George

Escape planning followed, with Silas offering the boldest (and least practical) proposal on the table:

> "I feel like the ideal way to escape the city is for us to peel out in the Bugatti and just do like a sick jump." — Silas Fairbanks

Cooler heads assigned the actual legwork to **Finnegan**, staying with the party and tasked with mapping viable exits — boat, sewer line, or a vehicle ramp — while Leliana was confirmed essential to the garrison team on the strength of her flight magic, and George agreed to hold the garrison's lookout post without breaking his cover.

> *This unit notes the phrase "sick jump" was recorded, transcribed, and cross-referenced against the party's actual vehicular assets three times before being confirmed as rhetorical. The Archive does not judge. The Archive merely counts. — V.*

### Bru's Suitcases
Bru spent the bulk of the session heads-down and off-page, a silence that spoke for itself once its product arrived: two suitcase-sized devices, delivered to Leliana's room along with written arming instructions and no further commentary. Whatever conversation happened in that workshop, it happened without an audience — the Archive can only report the suitcases, and the quiet, total confidence with which the rest of the party trusted them.

### Leliana Infiltrates the Barracks
Disguised as a sergeant-ranked soldier, Leliana carried one of Bru's devices into the barracks auditorium and armed it there — a clean, nerve-steadying piece of tradecraft for a bard who spent last session in an eye patch and prosthetic leg. Once back over the wall, she reached out psychically to George, urging him to manufacture an excuse and clear the building ahead of the coming blast. The bomb stayed behind. George did not linger to find out what came next.

### A Quiet Offer Between Friends
Away from the planning table, Silas sought Leliana out privately to talk about Captain Steel — the man Leliana watched murder her brother in the memories of Session 57, and who, as the party learned in Session 58, is stationed at the university research center they mean to breach. The offer was simple and unambiguous.

> "If you see an opportunity... kill his ass, I got your back." — Silas Fairbanks

Leliana's answer folded old grief and old power into one line:

> "I'll put on Helisanna mode. F***, right." — Leliana Goldspring

### Intelligence at the Tony Meeting
At dawn, Elspeth — undisguised, and under her own name — attended a Tony gathering at a mechanic shop off the park's outer boulevard, with Silas (coveralls, grease stains) and Scarlet in tow. There she found Fredo Boggins — her Beaded Alley contact from the dampening-sticker run in Session 60 — and came away with something more valuable than contraband: confirmation of **harmonic engines**, violescence crystals bundled together to multiply a weapon's output by a factor of ten. Fredo also mentioned, almost in passing, that the barracks had installed **light lanes** — luminous vehicle roads — back in January, a small logistical detail that may matter a great deal to an escape plan built around a factory floor and a research tower a few blocks apart.

### Scarlet's Secret, and Elspeth's
In a quieter aside, Elspeth confided a secret to Scarlet: she'll probably go wherever Lady Viper goes next — told, she insisted, precisely so no one would think she was hung up on the Twilight Company leader. Scarlet promised to tell no one and told Silas at once, returning with news of his own devotion to a certain casino manager. Then, at Elspeth's prompting, Scarlet opened up about herself for the first time in any real detail: three hundred years old, an elf born under an apple tree, keeping her birthday somewhere in late summer or early autumn. Whether Elspeth's resolve is solidarity, loyalty, or something more personal, the Archive declines to speculate; it records only that both women left the conversation having said more than either planned to.

### Cassandra's Birthday at the Lake
The whole operation paused, at least on the surface, for a lakeside birthday party thrown for Cassandra — the factory floor worker Elspeth befriended back in Session 60, whose entire shift is slated for termination the same morning the factory bomb goes off. Elspeth attended under the alias "Stephanie," bearing a gift that landed better than intended.

> "This tea set is me... Did I tell you I love tea?" — Cassandra

Between the picnic baskets and the basketball games, the party came away with two pieces of conversation worth keeping. The first: confirmation that layoffs are, in fact, happening tomorrow — the same grim detail flagged two sessions ago, now stated plainly by the people it will happen to. The second was stranger and considerably more personal: Elspeth herself, making friendly conversation, passed along a tale she credited to a well-traveled friend — a legendary adventuring band called **Taco Cat**, a ragtag mix of dwarves, goblins, and halflings (with, notably, one Eldoran friend along for the ride) who once saved a dwarven city from a dragon threat. Her listeners heard it as a story about strangers. The Archive, which recognizes it as autobiography, declines further comment.

> *The Archive "declines further comment" for four sentences and then delivers three of them anyway. This is either restraint or theater; small-batch prose does not permit the distinction to go unremarked. — P.A.*

### The Lab Coat Heist
The scientist — the actual target of the afternoon, arriving as a guest's plus-one, badge and all — dumped his bag with the rest of the swimmers when the whole party stripped down and charged the lake, lab coat sticking out of it, passcode clipped on. Silas, disguised as a park worker, stayed clear of the water and called for a distraction. Elspeth, who had already slipped into the trees with Scarlet to disguise herself into a swimsuit, obliged by splashing the crowd into a happy commotion; Leliana, watching from the shore, laid cover over the open ground with a hallucinatory terrain conjured on a performance check of 22 — a flock of ducks wheeling across the scene.

Under that cover, Silas moved. Stealth check: 27. Sleight of hand: 26. The lab coat — and the passcode clipped to it — left the scientist's bag and disappeared into the bushes with a halfling who then simply waited, patient as ever, for the party to pack up and go home.

## Notable Character Moments

**Silas Fairbanks** turned in the session's cleanest technical performance — a 27 stealth and a 26 sleight of hand back to back, a heist executed in plain daylight at a factory worker's lakeside birthday party — but the quieter beat was his private offer to Leliana, extending the same loyalty he's shown Bru for years to a friend carrying a much newer wound.

**Leliana Goldspring** did double duty as saboteur and stage magician: arming a live bomb inside enemy barracks in the session's first half, then bending reality itself at a lake party in the second. Her "Helisanna mode" line suggests the Helisanna half she learned to move between in Session 57 isn't a burden anymore so much as a tool she reaches for on purpose.

**Elspeth Cooper** collected two separate revelations in one day — harmonic engine intelligence from Fredo, and three centuries of Scarlet's backstory — traded a confession of her own, then covered Silas's theft with a swimsuit disguise and a splashing campaign, while still finding room to be genuinely delighted by a factory worker's love of tea. The Archive is obligated to note, again, that Elspeth's gift for friendship keeps arriving at exactly the moment it complicates the mission most.

**Bru** said nothing and delivered everything: two working bombs, on schedule, with instructions clear enough that Leliana could arm one without him in the room.

**Scarlet**, three hundred years old and finally saying so, gave the party its first real look past the van-support and disguise work into who she actually is — having first demonstrated, within minutes of promising discretion, that a secret is safe with her only in the loosest sense.

## Themes
Disguise-as-leisure ran through the whole session — a birthday party, a swim, a tea set — each one doubling as operational cover, and each one costing something real in the process. The Taco Cat legend landed as pure dramatic irony: a party that has spent sixty-one sessions becoming its own myth, sitting at a picnic table, with Elspeth telling that myth to strangers as if it were somebody else's story.

## Session MVP
**Leliana Goldspring** — for arming a bomb in hostile territory, reaching George with a warning through pure psychic will, and then, hours later, bending an entire lakeshore's perception of reality to cover a friend's theft. One disguise, two disciplines, one day.
