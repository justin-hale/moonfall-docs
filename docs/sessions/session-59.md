## Plot Events

### The Toothy's Morning Briefing
The session opened in the converted-church safehouse at 238, where [Olivia](/player-characters/olivia) — narrating into a tape recorder over coffee and a waffle, in full retired-detective mode — reviewed the day's objective: reconnaissance on the spaceport factory ahead of the coordinated three-target strike promised to George. Discussion turned practical and slightly paranoid, with the party debating how best to smuggle sensitive magical items past the city's new magic-detection helmets, including hiding Astro's orb in a lead box fitted with a "pee hole" for detection evasion.

### Disguises for the Harbor District
With disguise magic in hand, the party reshaped itself for the trip into the factory district. [Silas](/player-characters/silas) took the form of a middle-class human child, smooth and unassuming as ever. [Bru](/player-characters/bru), never one to pass up an opportunity for theater, copied Silas's disguise but layered a full Colonel Sanders costume over it — white suit, cane, wide hat, and a bearded child's face wearing an accent that wandered somewhere between Australian and deep Southern for the rest of the session. [Olivia](/player-characters/olivia), operating under her established "Ben Boulage" retired-detective persona, transformed further still into a tall, voluptuous older blonde woman, built specifically to sell a cover story: a first date, set up to disguise "the biggest explosion this side of the Mississippi" as pillow talk.

> "Do you want me to come with you, or are you going to like, shock me?" — Silas, to an unimpressed overseer

### The Wright-to-Boeing Skyline
Approaching the harbor, the party got its first unobstructed look at the Eldoran spaceport: rocket ships, barges, fighters, and impossible aircraft descending onto floating platforms, a scale of engineering the party had never before witnessed. Silas, characteristically unsentimental about the empire's achievements, offered the session's driest line on the subject of imperial progress.

> "The Elders got *something* out of all that genocide they committed." — Silas Fairbanks

### The Hairy Monkey and the Man Named Bon Bonnery
The party's destination was the Hairy Monkey — a restaurant run by a perpetually high proprietor named Harry (full name Harold) and staffed by an actual, sentient, extremely strong monkey referred to simply as "Monkey." "Ben Boulage" secured a table under the pretense of a romantic evening with a factory actor named Bon Bonnery — the party's inside man, proudly displaying an autographed photo of himself ("My very first autograph, Bon Bonner"). Over mixed nuts and coded, heavily suggestive dinner conversation, Bon Bonnery passed along intelligence about the factory floor. Meanwhile, Bru — still fully committed to the bearded Colonel Sanders bit — was pulled aside by Harry, who found the tiny impersonator "cool" and waved Monkey off to give the visitors whatever they wanted.

### Into the Furnace Factory
Disguised as fast-food delivery personnel, the party slipped into the spaceport factory proper, tracing the assembly line from raw materials to finished spacecraft components. At the far end sat the furnace — the intended target — locked behind a magically warded door requiring the factory overseer's key. [Silas](/player-characters/silas) scouted the structure with characteristic grace, scaling the factory walls with a stealth check of 32 (19 on the die, boosted by a hefty +13) before timing a leap onto the moving, spherical foreman's pod — itself gliding along ceiling tracks throughout the factory floor — clearing the DC 15 acrobatics check with room to spare. Bru and Olivia held position near a ventilation shaft dubbed, inevitably, "the pee hole," while a factory-wide hand-washing break (triggered in part by Silas's insistence that no one touch Admiral Meatloaf's caddy-delivered meatloaf with dirty hands) provided useful cover.

> "I'm not serving a lick of meatloaf until people here go wash their hands." — Silas Fairbanks

### The Overseer's Control Room
Silas rode the pod into a control room dominated by a heavyset, red-haired overseer of possible dwarven ancestry, reading a novel titled *The Stolen Chicken* by Margaret Batwood between shifts. The room held the keys the party needed, a bank of security monitors trained on the furnace and surrounding floor, and — more usefully — a half-finished beer and a bottle of sleeping pills sitting within easy reach.

### A Beer, a Sleeping Pill, and a Loud Mistake
With a natural 20 perception check (26 total) confirming the coast looked clear, Silas attempted to use mage hand to crush sleeping pills into the overseer's beer — a sleight-of-hand check of 16 that succeeded at the drugging but not at the silence, alerting the overseer to the disturbance.

> "What the godamn hell are y'all doing? Get back to work." — The Overseer

The party retreated without blowing its cover, regrouping to finalize the operation's shape: rather than staggering detonations and risking heightened security at the two remaining targets, they resolved to plant all three bombs and trigger them simultaneously. That decision came with its own internal debate — Bru's synchronized remote detonators versus Victor's older timer-based designs, a disagreement that was not close.

> "Brew is just the superior bomb maker. I avoid talking to Victor as much as possible." — Tyram (Silas's player)

## Notable Character Moments
- **Silas Fairbanks** carried the infiltration on his back this session — a 32 stealth check up the factory wall, a clean acrobatic leap onto a moving target, and a nat-20 perception roll that kept the whole operation invisible one beat longer than it had any right to be. Even his one loud misstep, the mage-hand beer job, landed the sleeping pills exactly where they needed to go.
- **Bru** committed fully to the Colonel Sanders bit for the entire session, charming Harry into cooperation through sheer novelty, and staked his professional pride on his own detonator designs over Victor's.
- **Olivia**, operating under her "Ben Boulage" alias, sold an entire fake courtship to extract intelligence from Bon Bonnery, proving once again that retirement never quite took.

## Themes
This session leaned into espionage-as-comedy: disguises stacked on disguises, a factory contact wooed over mixed nuts, and a Colonel Sanders impression carrying real tactical weight. Underneath the humor sat something colder — the spaceport's staggering technological leap, built, as Silas noted, on the backs of atrocity. The party's growing comfort with each other's methods (Bru's bombs over Victor's, Silas's improvisation over caution) also marked a quiet vote of confidence in each other ahead of a three-target strike with no room for error.

> *One admires the commitment to the Colonel Sanders bit, though this reader confesses a longing for prose that lingers less on stealth totals and more on the peculiar poetry of a goblin in a white suit charming a stoned restaurateur. Numbers are not, in themselves, a terroir. — P.A.*
