## Plot Events

### The Toothy's Morning Briefing
The session opened in the converted-church safehouse at 238, where [Olivia](/player-characters/olivia) — narrating into a tape recorder over coffee and a waffle, in full retired-detective mode — reviewed the day's objective: reconnaissance on the spaceport factory ahead of the coordinated three-target strike promised to George. Discussion turned practical and slightly paranoid, with the party debating how best to smuggle sensitive magical items past the city's new magic-detection helmets, including hiding Astro's orb in a lead box fitted with a "pee hole" for detection evasion.

### Disguises for the Harbor District
With disguise magic in hand, the party reshaped itself for the trip into the factory district. [Silas](/player-characters/silas) took the form of a middle-class human child, smooth and unassuming as ever. [Bru](/player-characters/bru), never one to pass up an opportunity for theater, copied Silas's disguise but layered a full Colonel Sanders costume over it — white suit, cane, wide hat, and a bearded child's face wearing an accent that wandered somewhere between Australian and deep Southern for the rest of the session. The session's most dramatic transformation, however, belonged to no one in the party. **Ben Boulage** — the private detective escorting Taco Cat to the meet, and the man who had arranged it — tipped the brim of his fedora and became a tall, voluptuous older blonde woman with flowing hair, without breaking stride or the conversation. "I'm a private detective," he offered, by way of explanation. "It's good for my business. Standard cover." [Olivia](/player-characters/olivia), the party's own retired detective, walked to the harbor as herself; her turn under a disguise came later, at the factory gate.

> "Do you want me to come with you, or are you going to like, shock me?" — Silas, to an unimpressed overseer

### The Wright-to-Boeing Skyline
Approaching the harbor, the party got its first unobstructed look at the Eldoran spaceport: rocket ships, barges, fighters, and impossible aircraft descending onto floating platforms, a scale of engineering the party had never before witnessed. Silas, characteristically unsentimental about the empire's achievements, offered the session's driest line on the subject of imperial progress.

> "The Elders got *something* out of all that genocide they committed." — Silas Fairbanks

### The Hairy Monkey and the Man Named Bon Bonnery
The party's destination was the Hairy Monkey — a restaurant run by a perpetually high proprietor named Harry (full name Harold) and staffed by an actual, sentient, extremely strong monkey referred to simply as "Monkey." Boulage secured a table for a romantic evening with a factory actor named Bon Bonnery — the party's inside man, whom Boulage had never met in person but could identify from an autographed photo he carried ("My very first autograph, Bon Bonner"). [Silas](/player-characters/silas) and [Olivia](/player-characters/olivia) took a nearby table, ordered nothing, and eavesdropped through a dinner conversation so heavily coded it barely qualified as code: a key that must be found *inside*, an overseer who wanders the floor carrying it, and a furnace that would "ignite this city like it's never seen this side of the Mississippi." Boulage and Bonnery then swept out together, spilling mixed nuts, still in character. Meanwhile, Bru — still fully committed to the bearded Colonel Sanders bit — was pulled aside by Harry, who found the tiny impersonator "cool" and waved Monkey off to give the visitors whatever they wanted.

### Into the Furnace Factory
Disguised as fast-food delivery personnel — Silas riding on Bru's shoulders to sell a full-height Colonel Sanders, Olivia fronting the crew as Wendy behind the food cart — the party slipped into the spaceport factory proper, tracing the assembly line from raw materials to finished spacecraft components. At the far end sat the furnace — the intended target — locked behind a magically warded door requiring the factory overseer's key. [Silas](/player-characters/silas) scouted the structure with characteristic grace, scaling the factory walls with a stealth check of 32 (19 on the die, boosted by a hefty +13) before timing a leap onto the moving, spherical foreman's pod — itself gliding along ceiling tracks throughout the factory floor — clearing the DC 15 acrobatics check with room to spare. Bru and Olivia held position near a ventilation shaft dubbed, inevitably, "the pee hole," while a factory-wide hand-washing break (triggered in part by Silas's insistence that no one touch Admiral Meatloaf's caddy-delivered meatloaf with dirty hands) provided useful cover.

> "I'm not serving a lick of meatloaf until people here go wash their hands." — Silas Fairbanks

### The Overseer's Control Room
Silas rode the pod into a control room dominated by a heavyset, red-haired overseer of possible dwarven ancestry, reading a novel titled *The Stolen Chicken* by Margaret Batwood between shifts. The room held the keys the party needed, a bank of security monitors trained on the furnace and surrounding floor, and — more usefully — a half-finished beer and a bottle of sleeping pills sitting within easy reach.

### A Beer, a Sleeping Pill, and a Loud Mistake
With a natural 20 perception check (26 total) confirming the coast looked clear, Silas attempted to use mage hand to crush sleeping pills into the overseer's beer — a sleight-of-hand check of 16 that succeeded at the drugging but not at the silence, alerting the overseer to the disturbance.

> "What the godamn hell are y'all doing? Get back to work." — The Overseer

The party retreated without blowing its cover, regrouping to finalize the operation's shape: rather than staggering detonations and risking heightened security at the two remaining targets, they resolved to plant all three bombs and trigger them simultaneously. That decision came with its own internal debate — Bru's synchronized remote detonators versus Victor's older timer-based designs, a disagreement that was not close.

> "Bru is just the superior bomb maker. I avoid talking to Victor as much as possible." — Taylor Ramsey (Silas's player)

## Notable Character Moments
- **Silas Fairbanks** carried the infiltration on his back this session — a 32 stealth check up the factory wall, a clean acrobatic leap onto a moving target, and a nat-20 perception roll that kept the whole operation invisible one beat longer than it had any right to be. Even his one loud misstep, the mage-hand beer job, landed the sleeping pills exactly where they needed to go.
- **Bru** committed fully to the Colonel Sanders bit for the entire session, charming Harry into cooperation through sheer novelty, and staked his professional pride on his own detonator designs over Victor's.
- **Olivia**, the party's actual retired detective, worked the Hairy Monkey the way a detective would: no disguise, no theatrics, a table within earshot of Boulage's cover date, and every useful word of it collected. At the factory gate she fronted the delivery crew as Wendy — the strongest of the three, and the only one not standing on someone else's shoulders.

## Themes
This session leaned into espionage-as-comedy: disguises stacked on disguises, a factory contact wooed over mixed nuts, and a Colonel Sanders impression carrying real tactical weight. Underneath the humor sat something colder — the spaceport's staggering technological leap, built, as Silas noted, on the backs of atrocity. The party's growing comfort with each other's methods (Bru's bombs over Victor's, Silas's improvisation over caution) also marked a quiet vote of confidence in each other ahead of a three-target strike with no room for error.

> *One admires the commitment to the Colonel Sanders bit, though this reader confesses a longing for prose that lingers less on stealth totals and more on the peculiar poetry of a goblin in a white suit charming a stoned restaurateur. Numbers are not, in themselves, a terroir. — P.A.*

## Session MVP
**Silas Fairbanks** — for a night of near-flawless infiltration: scaling walls, leaping onto moving targets, and reading a room (quite literally, at a natural 20) better than anyone else at the table.

Four passages amended, two summary lines rewritten, one quotation restored to its correct speaker and spelling, and one session date corrected by fourteen days.
