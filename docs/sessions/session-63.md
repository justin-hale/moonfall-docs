## Plot Events

### Escape Plans and the World Tree
Before committing to the day's operations, the party reviewed its exit strategy for after the three bombs went off: jump the Bugatti over the city's edge via ramp, escape by boat, or descend into the sewers. A fourth option was tabled as a last resort — a magical world tree that had sprouted from nothing to fifty or sixty feet in the park over the span of two days, still tethered to the same overgrown dimension that had once housed a serial-killer grandmother and a swamp. The tree's home-side had turned lush and verdant rather than barren, which nobody found reassuring. It was ranked, by consensus, a D-tier escape option — theoretically available, actively discouraged.

### Character Threads: Leliana's Balance, Elspeth's New Friends, and Scarlet's Time Gun
Quieter business preceded the raid. Asked at the table how Leliana's split personality was manifesting, Luke Neverisky (Leliana's player) answered that for now it works at will: her two personas — Leliana and Helisanna — are aware of each other, the change is "very controlled… just flipping a switch," and rather than fighting for control, the two seem to be cohabiting. Catching Olivia up on the lakeside birthday party, Silas reported that Elspeth had been on a roll making friends in the city, splashing in the lake with Cassandra's crowd of factory workers, and had come into her own since the party entered the fascist state. And the party quietly agreed to be gentler with Scarlet, whose inventive streak was drifting toward something called a time gun — a device rumored, in an earlier draft of the multiverse, to have destroyed an entire alternate universe. Nobody wanted to find out what a second draft looked like.

### The Factory Heist: Stealing the Sun
Bru, disguised as a courtly Colonel Sanders — polished shoes, Van Dyke mustache, white hair, cane, black tie — and Olivia, hatted and folded into a skeleton-crew shift change, walked into the spaceport factory to plant the two-stage bomb built to breach the furnace casing and drop a second payload onto the power core. A failed attempt to reach Silas by message spell (the range fell just short of a mile, and luck was not with the dice) left the pair without backup, so they talked their way into cover roles instead: Olivia, on a strong persuasion check, convinced the foreman they were reinforcements sent to cover a scheduling mishap. Arcana checks placed Bru as an "entropy mechanic" bound for the furnace interior and Olivia as a "smelter" assigned to cast-plate changing.

Suited against the heat, Bru entered the furnace chamber and found something closer to a miniature sun than a power core — a sphere of raw energy roughly the size of a school bus. Winged boots carried him to the top, where a natural 20 on an acrobatics check let him plant the internal bomb, wired with hair-trigger seismic sensors, exactly where it needed to go.

And then Bru had a better idea.

Rather than destroy the core, he cast *Reduce* on it, shrinking the sun down to roughly two feet across, and pulled it into a *Rope Trick* pocket to buy himself an hour. He climbed out insisting the core had gotten "really, really big," then "really, really small," and was simply gone — a collapse, as the foreman reported it — and held the story together under direct questioning from the foreman and a coworker named Tuni. When the pair tried to take it to Captain Steel, a voice from behind his office door told them to go away. Steel came out only later, berating Olivia as an idiot, tearing the furnace door open one-handed, and flying off. Outside, he came bursting through a door and bolted toward Bru, but Bru saw him a split second first and vanished with *Misty Step*. Earlier, Bru had bought the foreman's cooperation with a shared drink and a persuasion check of 18, insisting that "brown" was flammable enough to substitute for a furnace, while Olivia used the resulting chaos to scale the furnace exterior and plant the casing bomb on top. Along the way, she also talked an opportunistic thief already casing the factory into leaving quietly, trading his silence for hers.

> "Yes. I just pour the forges." — Olivia Cooper, mid-bluff, when Captain Steel asked whether she was an idiot

> "You're going to do this your whole life... looking back and being like, I did all this for this company." — Bru, to a burned-out foreman

> "The hot part's gone." — Olivia Cooper

> "Steel's freaking out. I gotta get the hell out of here." — Olivia Cooper

### Containing a Star: Scarlet's Secret Chest
With the borrowed hour on the *Rope Trick* running down, the party turned to Scarlet, whose *Leomund's Secret Chest* — a spell she'd apparently been waiting the better part of her three hundred years to justify casting — offered permanent, ethereal storage. The reduced sun went into the chest without incident, solving a problem the party hadn't planned to have: what do you do with a star once you've already stolen it.

> "I could kiss you right now, Scarlet, but I won't cuz you're disgusting." — Bru

### The Research Tower: Six Faces in the Simulators
While the factory job unfolded, the rest of the party — disguised in scientist coats, a Monopoly-man getup, and whatever else passed for university faculty — infiltrated the research tower to plant a remote-detonated charge inside the elevator shaft. Silas climbed the shaft's exterior via spider climb and set the bomb on the elevator roof.

The floor's contents were worse than expected. Six flight simulators held six sleeping copies of Elspeth Cooper, being trained as shock troopers for the Empire's war against the dragons. Silas recalled, grimly, that the last set of Elspeth-shaped test subjects the party had encountered had been brainwashed rather than irreversibly altered — meaning these six might still be saved rather than simply destroyed.

> "They're training Elspeth to be shock troopers in their war with the dragons." — Silas Fairbanks

### Evacuation, Detonation, and the Falling Star
To clear the building of bystanders before detonation, Olivia pulled the fire alarm on a clean sleight-of-hand check, and the tower emptied in the ensuing scramble. As the party made for the exits, something else caught their eye through the chaos: an unidentified starship, plummeting toward the earth in the distance — origin, purpose, and relevance all entirely unknown, and left, for now, an open question. The bomb went off on schedule. The tower came down, its wreckage sprawling across the city wall and spanning several blocks — considerably more collapse than anyone had budgeted for, and an unplanned gift to the evening's getaway.

### The Tower Against the Wall
The session closed with the research tower leaning against the city wall and Silas asking whether it was rampable. Leliana answered with a sweet, unplanned guitar riff, and there the night ended. The loudest available option was floated as the table packed up — drive the Bugatti off the tower, over the wall, with fireworks — and it waits on next session. Silas clapped Bru on the shoulder, giddy over the day's true prize.

> "I'm so happy for you right now, buddy." — Silas Fairbanks, to Bru

> "My vote is we drive the Bugatti off the tower over the wall and set off some sick fireworks and just blow this taco stand." — Silas Fairbanks

> "You blew that out of the water. They failed to get the moon — you got that goddamn sun in five minutes." — Silas Fairbanks

> "We still got to find another opportunity to get Helisanna's revenge." — Silas Fairbanks

The party's third target was down and the city was in rubble behind them, with Finnegan waiting at the docks for whichever escape route they actually needed.

## Notable Character Moments
- **Bru** turned a straightforward demolition job into the session's headline heist, choosing to steal the factory's stellar core rather than simply destroy it, and then lying convincingly to his foreman while carrying a shrunken sun in his pocket.
- **Olivia** handled the factory's social engineering almost single-handedly in Silas's absence — talking her way into a cover role, bluffing an unrelated thief off the premises, and scaling machinery she openly struggled to climb.
- **Silas** contributed the session's clearest emotional beat, expressing unguarded pride in Bru's improvisation and voicing the unresolved thread of Helisanna's unavenged wrongs.
- **Leliana** scouted the new World Tree in the guise of a child playing in its branches, and closed the session with a guitar flourish scored to a collapsing building.
- **Scarlet** got to use a spell she had apparently been saving for exactly this occasion, and the party — mindful of recent conversations about being kinder to her — let her have the moment.

## Themes
Improvisation over demolition ran through the entire session: nearly every plan (the message spell, the furnace bomb, the fire alarm) failed or mutated into something else, and the party's competence showed less in following the plan than in replacing it on the fly. The discovery of the Elspeth clones raised the session's most unsettling note — identity as something that can be manufactured, trained, and weaponized without its owner's consent — while the ambiguous starship overhead suggested the sabotage campaign's stakes extend well past the city walls.

## Session MVP
**Bru**, for walking into a bombing mission and walking out having stolen a star.
