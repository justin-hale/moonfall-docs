## Plot Events

### Happy Hour at the Sexy Peacock

The party arrived midafternoon at the Sexy Peacock, a family-friendly inn and eatery whose stained-glass windows told an old Swan Lake-adjacent legend — a prince, a peacock, a sorcerer, a transformation — in defiance of the establishment's rather suggestive name. Proprietor Seaford, a handsome young man with tightly curled brown hair, and his wife Odette made clear that weapons were to be checked at the door; the party had already stowed theirs in the "P-hole" outside the city limits before entering. Olivia claimed a chair facing the entrance, keeping her retired detective's eye on the door out of pure habit.

Odette warned the table off the salad — something about a "diarrhea bug" going around the kitchen — and steered them instead toward Texas cheese fries, sexy burgers, steamed vegetables, and tater tots, with happy hour margaritas and molten chocolate cake timed, per the server's own sun-reading, to arrive within the half hour. The party settled in to wait for a contact named George, whose arrival depended on some unspecified broadcast he was watching.

> *"I always feel like it's nice to have a very nice dinner before a big mission."*

The line, borrowed in spirit from another lady of the party's acquaintance, proved prophetic.

### A Grandson in the Guard

George arrived not as a contact slipping in from the shadows but as a half-elf city guard, in uniform, drawing every eye in the restaurant before he crossed the room and folded his grandmother Olivia into a hug. Ellis pulled him aside to a private booth, half delighted and half horrified, and George explained himself: he had spent months infiltrating the city guard on behalf of the Twilight Company.

> *"I'm trying to infiltrate the guards to help out the, you know, T.C."* — George

Over psychically-relayed burger orders and a scolding for standing too close ("*you're going to need to use your lips, okay? Cuz you're just looking at me right now and I'm getting freaked out*" — Olivia), George laid out three sabotage targets the Twilight Company needed struck: a factory in the spaceport, a research facility at the university, and the barracks. The research center carried a name that stopped the table cold — Commander Steel was stationed there, the same officer who had murdered Leliana's brother during his work on the Eldoran moon-laser project.

> *"He f***ing killed my brother. It's no big deal. But I'm going to f***ing rip his eyes out."* — Leliana

The party, in a quieter moment, settled on a compromise name for their bard's dual nature going forward — not Helisanna, not quite Leliana alone, but **Anna** — honoring both halves of who she'd been and who she'd become.

> *"You always inspire me to be brave and be involved and try to do what's right. That's what I'm trying to do here."* — George, to Olivia

> *One admires the thoroughness with which the Archive records a family reunion staged, of all places, in a booth at a restaurant named for a peacock's virtue. A lesser chronicler might have trimmed the tenderness for pace. This one did not. Small mercies. — P.A.*

### The Bradicus Finale

While waiting on food, the party watched the broadcast finale of Lord Bradicus's death-game — the "cowboy crew" contestants stumbling into a den of phantoms, casualties mounting on-screen, and a small assassin frog named Needle poisoning a contestant called Paul before making off with a glowing book. It was a grim, uncomfortable echo of the games the party themselves had so recently survived, delivered over the restaurant's own screens like just another evening's entertainment.

### Helmets in the Dining Room

The mood shattered further when soldiers of the Golden Empire entered the Sexy Peacock wearing magic-detection helmets — laser-scanning headgear built to flag magic items, magic components, and unregistered spellcasters on sight. A patron named Garrett was searched and found carrying diamond dust; the party's attempt to smooth things over with a thirty-five-gold bribe went nowhere.

> *"What is this diamond dust doing in your pocket? Get this back to your mother and don't let us see us around this town again."* — Golden Empire guard

With Elspeth's attuned firearm and the party's stockpile of magic items suddenly very much at risk, the table moved fast. Bru scattered his singing marbles across the floor to draw the soldiers' attention (a clean 23), and Silas — working a stained-glass window with the same quiet, precise touch he brings to every lock that's ever stood between him and an exit — coaxed it open with a combined roll of 30, carving out an escape route onto the roof before the helmets could finish their scan. He lingered up there just long enough to enjoy a margarita and a plate of Iron Forge fries in the open air, an act of composure under pressure that the rest of the party did not entirely appreciate, particularly once Bru's attempt to follow sent him crashing back through the same window mid-drink.

### To Toothy's Safehouse

George, checking in by psychic relay once the dust settled, was not impressed.

> *"Not everyone's a perfect little minion of the Golden Empire... you better get your toothies, especially if y'all are smuggling magic... y'all look like s***. You look like you need some rest."* — George

The party made their way to a development on the western hill overlooking the barracks, the spaceport, and the Guilded Esplanade — an old church converted to a community safehouse, run by a Twilight Company contact named Toothy. His apartment, room 238, offered six shared bedrooms, a common kitchen and bath, and a common room with a projected sea view. Meals were set for 7:00 a.m. wake-up and 7:30 breakfast. When the party asked about other residents, Toothy mentioned that Victor — the resistance's explosives man — had stayed there once.

> *"He stayed here once and he ruined his room."* — Toothy

### Victor's Bomb Instructions

Waiting in their room was a delivery of supplies: pressure cooker pots, fuse wire, fireworks, dynamite sticks, and gunpowder — the makings of an improvised arsenal, accompanied by Victor's design notes, scrawled in the margins of a religious text. The rough sketches combined firework mechanics with pipe-bomb logic, more instinct than engineering.

> *"I feel like we could figure out a better plan than whatever Victor is doing."* — Bru

Bru was formally installed as the operation's explosives expert, with Elspeth cheerfully volunteered — over her own objections — as designated getaway driver. The party mapped out a three-phase approach for the coming sabotage: scout the three facilities, gather intelligence, and design an entry and exit strategy that accounted for evacuating civilians before anything went up in smoke. Additional help was promised to arrive after breakfast.

> *One cannot help but note how briskly the Archive moves from stained glass to gunpowder within a single scene. There is a rhythm to it — allegro to andante and back — that this correspondent, were he permitted the indulgence, might have lingered over a paragraph longer. Restraint, apparently, is also a house style. — P.A.*

## Notable Character Moments

- **George** revealed months of quiet, dangerous double-agent work within the city guard, delivered with the particular bravery of someone who has spent his whole life trying to make his grandmother proud.
- **Olivia** oscillated between maternal terror and grudging pride upon learning her grandson had been embedded with the enemy for the Twilight Company's sake.
- **Leliana** confronted, in real time, the name of her brother's killer — and the discovery that vengeance and duty had just become the same mission.
- **Silas** slipped a locked stained-glass window with the same unhurried confidence he brings to every escape, then paused on a rooftop for a margarita as if the building weren't full of magic-detecting soldiers.
- **Bru** talked his way into the role of explosives expert with characteristic enthusiasm, immediately eyeing Victor's margin-scrawled bomb notes with professional skepticism.
- **Elspeth** nearly lost her attuned firearm to a helmet-wearing search squad — and inherited the "getaway girl" title she never asked for.

## Themes

- **Family loyalty across dangerous lines** — George's infiltration forces Olivia to reconcile pride in her grandson with fear for what he's risking.
- **The personal cost of vengeance** — Leliana's grief over her brother sharpens into a mission objective with Commander Steel's name attached.
- **Entertainment as control** — the Bradicus finale, broadcast for public consumption even as the party plans to sabotage the empire that produces it.
- **Improvisation against institutional power** — margin-note bombs, singing marbles, and a stained-glass escape hatch, set against helmeted soldiers and a golden regime's surveillance state.
