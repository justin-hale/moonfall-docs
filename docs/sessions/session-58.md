## Plot Events

### Happy Hour at the Sexy Peacock

The party arrived midafternoon at the Sexy Peacock, a family-friendly inn and eatery whose stained-glass windows of dancing peacocks — a story no one at the table could yet place — sat in quiet defiance of the establishment's rather suggestive name. Proprietor Seaford, a handsome young man with flowing, mid-length brown hair curled back, and his wife Odette made clear that weapons were to be kept in check; the party had already stowed theirs in the "P-hole," the portable hole Silas carries, while crossing the city. Olivia claimed a chair facing the entrance, keeping her retired detective's eye on the door out of pure habit.

Odette warned the table off the salad — something going around was making people sick — and recommended the mozzarella sticks; the table settled on Texas cheese fries, sexy burgers, steamed vegetables, and tater tots, with happy hour margaritas and molten chocolate cake timed, per the server's own sun-reading, to arrive within the half hour. The party settled in to wait for a contact named George, whose arrival depended on some unspecified broadcast he was watching.

> *"The word sexy has no meaning here."* — Bru

On the Sexy Peacock's menu, it emerged, "sexy" mostly meant bacon.

### A Grandson in the Guard

George arrived not as a contact slipping in from the shadows but as a city guard, in uniform, drawing every eye in the restaurant before he crossed the room and folded his grandmother Olivia into a hug. Olivia pulled him aside to a private booth, half delighted and half horrified, and George explained himself: he had spent months infiltrating the city guard on behalf of the Twilight Company.

> *"I'm trying to infiltrate the guards to help out the, you know, T.C."* — George

Over burgers — and one order George tried to place by psychic link alone, which earned him a firm correction from Odette ("*you're going to need to use your lips, okay? Cuz you're just looking at me right now and I'm getting freaked out*") — George laid out three sabotage targets the Twilight Company needed struck: a factory in the spaceport, a research facility at the university, and the barracks. The research center carried a name that stopped the table cold — Captain Steel was stationed there, the same officer who had murdered Leliana's brother during his work on the Eldoran moon-laser project.

> *"He f***ing killed my brother. It's no big deal. But I'm going to f***ing rip his eyes out."* — Leliana

In a quieter moment, Bru and Elspeth floated a single name to cover both halves of their bard — **Anna**. It did not stick: she remains Leliana or Helisanna, depending on which of her is out.

> *"You're always trying to look out for me. You always have... But you also inspire me to be brave and be involved and try to do what's right. That's what I'm trying to do here."* — George, to Olivia

> *One admires the thoroughness with which the Archive records a family reunion staged, of all places, in a booth at a restaurant named for a peacock's virtue. A lesser chronicler might have trimmed the tenderness for pace. This one did not. Small mercies. — P.A.*

### The Bradicus Finale

While waiting on food, the party watched the broadcast finale of Lord Bradicus's death-game — the "cowboy crew" contestants stumbling into a den of phantoms, casualties mounting on-screen, and a small assassin frog named Needle felling a contestant called Jesse with a poisoned blow dart before making off with a glowing book. It was a grim, uncomfortable echo of the games the party themselves had so recently survived, delivered over the restaurant's own screens like just another evening's entertainment.

### Helmets in the Dining Room

The mood shattered further when soldiers of the Golden Empire entered the Sexy Peacock wearing magic-detection helmets — laser-scanning headgear built to flag magic items, magic components, and unregistered spellcasters on sight. A patron named Garrett was searched and found carrying diamond dust; he and his companion bought their way out with a thirty-five-gold bribe scraped together at the table.

> *"What is this diamond dust doing in your pocket? Get this back to your mother and don't let us see us around this town again."* — Golden Empire guard

With the party's stockpile of magic items suddenly very much at risk — Silas, at least, already had everyone's weapons tucked away in the P-hole — the table moved fast. Bru scattered his singing marbles across the floor to draw the soldiers' attention (a clean 23), and Silas — working a stained-glass window with the same quiet, precise touch he brings to every lock that's ever stood between him and an exit — coaxed it open with a combined roll of 30, carving out an escape route onto the roof before the helmets could finish their scan. He lingered up there just long enough to enjoy a margarita and a plate of Iron Forge fries in the open air, an act of composure under pressure that the rest of the party did not entirely appreciate — particularly Bru, who had stayed behind in the booth and was caught reaching for Silas's margarita just as the halfling doubled back to claim it.

### To Toothy's Safehouse

George, checking in by psychic relay once the dust settled, was not impressed.

> *"Not everyone's a perfect little minion of the Golden Empire... you better get your toothies, especially if y'all are smuggling magic... y'all look like s***. You look like you need some rest."* — George

Before leaving, Olivia finally asked Odette about the name. The stained-glass windows told an old Swan Lake-adjacent legend — a prince, a flock of dancing peacocks, a sorcerer dressed as an owl — and the property had come to Seaford from his family as something rather less family-friendly; the couple kept the name because the whole city already knew it.

The party made their way to a development on the western hill, beside an old church turned community center, overlooking the barracks, the spaceport, and the Guilded Esplanade. There, in an apartment building, a Twilight Company contact named Toothy kept room 238 on the third floor: six bedrooms ringing a common room with a projected sea view, a shared kitchen and bath, and every bedroom but the two set aside for the party already taken. Meals were set for 7:00 a.m. wake-up and 7:30 breakfast. When the party asked about other residents, Toothy mentioned that Victor — the resistance's explosives man — had stayed there once.

> *"He stayed here once and he ruined his room."* — Toothy

### Victor's Bomb Instructions

Toothy produced Victor's design notes from his own pocket — scrawled in the margins of a page torn from a religious text — while waiting in the party's room was a delivery of supplies: pressure cooker pots, fuse wire, fireworks, dynamite sticks, and gunpowder, the makings of an improvised arsenal. The rough sketches combined firework mechanics with pipe-bomb logic, more instinct than engineering.

> *"I feel like we could figure out a better plan than whatever Victor is doing."* — Bru

Silas and Elspeth promptly appointed Bru the operation's explosives expert, and Elspeth — ceding the explosives title with good grace — claimed "getaway girl" for herself. The party mapped out phase one of the coming sabotage: scout the three facilities, gather intelligence, and work out an entry and exit strategy that accounted for evacuating civilians before anything went up in smoke. Additional help was promised to arrive after breakfast.

> *One cannot help but note how briskly the Archive moves from stained glass to gunpowder within a single scene. There is a rhythm to it — allegro to andante and back — that this correspondent, were he permitted the indulgence, might have lingered over a paragraph longer. Restraint, apparently, is also a house style. — P.A.*

## Notable Character Moments

- **George** revealed months of quiet, dangerous double-agent work within the city guard, delivered with the particular bravery of someone who has spent his whole life trying to make his grandmother proud.
- **Olivia** oscillated between maternal terror and grudging pride upon learning her grandson had been embedded with the enemy for the Twilight Company's sake.
- **Leliana** confronted, in real time, the name of her brother's killer — and the discovery that vengeance and duty had just become the same mission.
- **Silas** slipped out a stained-glass window with the same unhurried confidence he brings to every escape, then paused on a rooftop for a margarita as if the building weren't full of magic-detecting soldiers.
- **Bru** was handed the role of explosives expert by his friends' acclamation, immediately eyeing Victor's margin-scrawled bomb notes with professional skepticism.
- **Elspeth** gave up her claim on the explosives title with good grace — and named herself "getaway girl," a signature, as Silas pointed out, best worn with an explosion in the rearview.

## Themes

- **Family loyalty across dangerous lines** — George's infiltration forces Olivia to reconcile pride in her grandson with fear for what he's risking.
- **The personal cost of vengeance** — Leliana's grief over her brother sharpens into a mission objective with Captain Steel's name attached.
- **Entertainment as control** — the Bradicus finale, broadcast for public consumption even as the party plans to sabotage the empire that produces it.
- **Improvisation against institutional power** — margin-note bombs, singing marbles, and a stained-glass escape hatch, set against helmeted soldiers and a golden regime's surveillance state.
