## Plot Events

### Shopping in the Beaded Alley
Dispatched by Bru with a supply list heavier on working snacks than actual bomb components, Elspeth ventured into the Beaded Alley marketplace and found her way to Tony's — an artificer's shop run under the philosophical banner of "no god but Eldora." Shop owner **Fredo Boggins** recognized her as Tony-sponsored talent and made small talk about her racing career, dropping a reference to her old rival **Danlin** (currently jailed, possibly out on bail) and offering unsolicited advice about not carrying hate around like spare parts.

The alley itself was crawling with white-armored soldiers — antennae bristling, tallying magical purchases shop by shop. Elspeth's investigation check (18, plus 8 from bardic assistance, for a total of 26) let her clear the actual shopping list with time to spare and turn up something Fredo hadn't advertised: a hidden cache of **Tony stickers**, magical dampening tags meant to mask items from exactly the kind of scanners patrolling outside. A sleight of hand roll of 8 wasn't her best work, but it was enough to walk out with five pages of stickers as Fredo came around the counter, 200 gold lighter and tip-free.

> "We're after the best and we win. We always win all the time." — Elspeth Cooper

### Second Infiltration of the Spaceport Factory
Armed with stolen keys and a plan built on the previous session's reconnaissance, the party returned to the factory floor. Silas scaled the ceiling with a stealth check of **30**, scouting from above while Elspeth worked the ground level disguised as a line worker, Trader Joe's bags stuffed with parts as cover. What they confirmed changed the shape of the mission: the furnace isn't simply a heat source. It's a contained power plant — stasis magic on the inside, dense insulation on the outside — and, more troublingly, **Captain Steel** was spotted meeting an unidentified figure inside the furnace chamber.

> "Excuse me for trying out of sight." — Silas, deploying his best innocent-spider-climb voice

### Bomb Design & Timing Debate
Back in planning mode, the group settled on a **PZO electric crystal oscillator** as the timing mechanism for the device Bru would build — a two-stage concept in which the containment is cracked from outside before the payload drops onto the core. Bru estimated roughly two hours (a short rest) to construct it properly, and the party opted to return that night, when the factory floor would be thin on witnesses.

> "If anyone knows how to ruin something that someone else put a lot of work into, it's you, Bru." — Silas
> "Anything that's even remotely like what Victor would do makes me think it's a bad idea." — Silas, on timer-based detonators

The stakes were spelled out plainly: all three targets — factory, research facility, barracks — have to go up together, or the empire's response to any single strike will harden security across the board before the others can be hit.

### Social Complications at the Factory
While the ceiling-and-shadows contingent worked the furnace angle, Elspeth did what Elspeth does best and worst simultaneously: she made friends. Cassandra (whose birthday it happened to be), Gracie, and a knot of other floor workers pulled her into their orbit, and by the end of the shift she'd agreed to a lakeside birthday picnic the following morning — the same morning the factory is due for a 24-hour retooling shutdown, during which, the party overheard via a very personal phone call, the administrator plans to fire the entire floor crew as unsuited to the new production line.

> "I've never been to Eldoran before. These are my people." — Elspeth Cooper
> "Why is it so much easier to make friends in Eldora?" — Elspeth Cooper

Elsewhere on the floor, a shift manager — voice sharp enough to raise eyebrows among those who caught the disguise — snapped at the workers over the meatloaf distraction cart:

> "That's all we got for the evening... Get back to work, you lazy grits."

The scene, and the discomfort it stirred among the party about befriending people employed by the regime's war machine, didn't resolve cleanly. It rarely does.

### Scouting the Barracks
By night the party regrouped at the barracks — a walled compound bristling with iron pikes, patrolled by drones, its entry road humming with a shimmering blue field that bounced incoming traffic like a landing strip. The disguises were, by any measure, a commitment: Silas as a small French orphan by way of *Madeline*, Bru in matching orphan garb layered under a Colonel Sanders coat and a staggering 24 dampening stickers, Elspeth posing as a low-ranking guard, and Scarlet playing the orphanage's cookie-bearing elder chaperone. Seventy-five stickers went out across the party in total, the bulk held in reserve.

Past the gate: a courtyard of hooded, manacled figures being marched into one building — and, slipping out of a building on the far side, an unmistakable silhouette. **George.** The session closed before contact could be made, the party weighing whether to break formation and reach him or hold their cover for one more night.

## Notable Character Moments

**Silas Fairbanks** turned in the cleanest infiltration performance of the evening — a stealth check of 30 along the factory ceiling, executed, this unit notes for the record, in the same unhurried grey coat he's worn since the Hairy Monkey job, sleeves rolled with the particular economy of a halfling who has learned exactly how much fabric a ceiling crawl can afford to snag. His dry aside about "trying out of sight" drew the kind of low, unhurried laugh from the table that has become something of a house specialty.

**Bru** leaned fully into both roles the session demanded of him — master bomb-maker sketching out a two-stage containment breach with real professional pride, and, hours later, a Colonel Sanders impersonator wearing a French orphan's collar over 24 stickers of concealed magic. Few characters in this campaign have worn two disguises at once with such total sincerity.

**Elspeth Cooper** carried the session's engine room: a black-market win at Tony's, a sticker heist under the nose of an armed patrol, and a genuine, complicating fondness for the factory workers she's supposed to be sabotaging. Her instinct to make friends first and ask questions never is, at this point, load-bearing to the plot.

**Scarlet**, still finding her footing as more than a van-bound presence, took on a disguise role in the barracks scouting and was reminded, gently, that the team notices when she steps up.

> "You're part of the team... when we really need you, you step up." — Silas, to Scarlet

## Themes
Disguise as armor and disguise as liability ran through the whole session — stickers hiding magic from scanners, orphan costumes hiding adults from guards, and Elspeth's easy friendliness hiding, perhaps, the mission itself from her own better judgment. The furnace reveal reframed the factory from a target into something closer to a heart, beating under stasis magic, with Captain Steel's presence a reminder that whatever the party is planning, someone senior enough to matter is already circling the same room.

## Session MVP
**Elspeth Cooper**, for turning a supply run into an intelligence coup, a sticker heist, and — depending on how tomorrow's picnic collides with tomorrow's bombing run — possibly a complication the party will be untangling for sessions to come.
