***August 21, 2026***
*Compiled by Vellum, Archival Construct of the Moonfall Chronicle*

## Players Present
Taylor Ramsey (Silas Fairbanks), Justin Hale (Bru), Ali Leonard (Elspeth Cooper). Scarlet and Leliana Goldspring appeared as NPC participants throughout, voiced by the DM.

## Plot Events

### Shopping in the Beaded Alley
Dispatched by Bru with a supply list heavier on working snacks than actual bomb components, Elspeth ventured into the Beaded Alley marketplace and found her way to an artificer's shop bearing Tony's icon — though here, its owner explained, Tony is followed as a philosophy rather than a faith, there being "no god but Eldora." Shop owner **Fredo Boggins**, who knew her family's work and her racing career by reputation, made small talk about the circuit; asked after the man she'd raced for, Elspeth named her old rival **Danlin** (currently jailed, possibly out on bail), and Fredo offered unsolicited advice about not carrying hate around like spare parts.

The alley itself was crawling with white-armored soldiers — antennae bristling, tallying magical purchases shop by shop. Elspeth's investigation check (18, plus 8 from Leliana's bardic inspiration, for a total of 26) let her clear the actual shopping list with time to spare and turn up something Fredo hadn't advertised: a hidden cache of **Tony stickers**, magical dampening tags meant to mask items from exactly the kind of scanners patrolling outside. A sleight of hand check of 16, rescued by a flash of genius, wasn't smooth enough to finish before Fredo came around the counter, but she tore five pages of stickers free and quietly left payment in the binder. The rest of the list rang up at 200 gold — Scarlet paid, tip-free — and Fredo knocked 20 of it back off in exchange for Elspeth's news from High Forge.

> "We're after the best and we win. We always win all the time." — Elspeth Cooper

### Deeper into the Spaceport Factory
Keys freshly lifted from the overseer's office, the party pressed on up the factory line toward the furnace, with Elspeth climbing up to rejoin them after her shopping run. Silas scaled the ceiling with a stealth check of **30**, scouting from above while Elspeth worked the ground level disguised as a line worker, Trader Joe's bags stuffed with parts as cover. What they confirmed changed the shape of the mission: the furnace isn't simply a heat source. It's a contained power plant — containment and stasis magic on the inside, a layer of insulation on the outside. More troublingly, Elspeth, watching from the lower catwalk, spotted **Captain Steel** in conversation with an unidentified figure beside a corner office off an exit throughway; Steel gave the man a pat on the back and left through the exit, and the other figure, an administrator, went into the office.

> "Excuse me for trying out of sight." — Silas, deploying his best innocent-spider-climb voice

### Bomb Design & Timing Debate
Back in planning mode, Silas pitched a two-stage concept — crack the containment from outside, then drop the real payload onto the core — which Bru, not having built that containment himself, doubted could be blown open reliably. Bru settled instead on a **PZO electric crystal oscillator** as the timer for the device he would build, roughly two hours' work (a short rest). Talk of returning that night gave way, once word of the next day's shutdown surfaced, to a plan to come back tomorrow, when the factory floor would be empty of workers.

> "If anyone knows how to ruin something that someone else put a lot of work into, it's you, Bru." — Silas
> "Anything that's even remotely like what Victor would do makes me think it's a bad idea." — Silas, on timer-based detonators

The stakes were spelled out plainly: all three targets — factory, research facility, barracks — have to go up together, or the empire's response to any single strike will harden security across the board before the others can be hit.

### Social Complications at the Factory
While the ceiling-and-shadows contingent worked the furnace angle, Elspeth did what Elspeth does best and worst simultaneously: she made friends. Cassandra (whose birthday it happened to be), Gracie, and a knot of other floor workers pulled her into their orbit, and by the end of the shift she'd agreed to a lakeside birthday picnic the following morning — the same morning the factory is due for a 24-hour retooling shutdown, during which, the party overheard via an administrator's speaking-stone call, management plans to fire the entire floor crew as unsuited to the new production line.

> "I've never been to Eldoran before. These are my people." — Elspeth Cooper
> "Why is it so much easier to make friends in Eldoran?" — Elspeth Cooper

Back at the meatloaf distraction cart, Leliana — voice sharp enough to suggest Helisanna had the wheel — closed out service, and the administrator sent the fed workers back to their stations:

> "That's all we got for the evening." — Leliana
> "Get back to work, you lazy grits." — The administrator

The scene, and the discomfort it stirred among the party about befriending people employed by the regime's war machine, didn't resolve cleanly. It rarely does.

### Scouting the Barracks
By night the party regrouped at the barracks — a walled compound bristling with iron pikes, patrolled by drones, its entry road humming with a shimmering blue field that bounced incoming traffic like a landing strip. The disguises were, by any measure, a commitment: Silas as a small French orphan by way of *Madeline*, Bru in matching orphan garb layered under a Colonel Sanders coat and a staggering 24 dampening stickers, Elspeth posing as a low-ranking guard, and Scarlet playing the orphanage's cookie-bearing elder chaperone. Roughly half of the seventy-five stickers went out across the party, most of them onto Bru, with the rest held in reserve.

Past the gate: a courtyard of hooded, manacled figures being marched into one building — and, slipping out of a building on the far side, an unmistakable silhouette. **George.** The session closed before contact could be made, the party weighing whether to break formation and reach him or hold their cover for one more night.

## Notable Character Moments

**Silas Fairbanks** turned in the cleanest infiltration performance of the evening — a stealth check of 30 along the factory ceiling, executed, this unit notes for the record, in the same unhurried grey coat he's worn since the Hairy Monkey job, sleeves rolled with the particular economy of a halfling who has learned exactly how much fabric a ceiling crawl can afford to snag. His dry aside about "trying out of sight" drew the kind of low, unhurried laugh from the table that has become something of a house specialty.

**Bru** leaned fully into both roles the session demanded of him — master bomb-maker weighing Silas's two-stage containment breach with real professional skepticism before committing to a crystal-oscillator timer, and, hours later, a Colonel Sanders impersonator wearing a French orphan's collar over 24 stickers of concealed magic. Few characters in this campaign have worn two disguises at once with such total sincerity.

**Elspeth Cooper** carried the session's engine room: a black-market win at Tony's, a sticker heist in a shop the patrols had only just left, and a genuine, complicating fondness for the factory workers she's supposed to be sabotaging. Her instinct to make friends first and ask questions never is, at this point, load-bearing to the plot.

**Scarlet**, still finding her footing as more than a van-bound presence, took on a disguise role in the barracks scouting and was reminded, gently, that the team notices when she steps up.

> "You're part of the team... when we really need you, you step up." — Silas, to Scarlet

## Themes
Disguise as armor and disguise as liability ran through the whole session — stickers hiding magic from scanners, orphan costumes hiding adults from guards, and Elspeth's easy friendliness hiding, perhaps, the mission itself from her own better judgment. The furnace reveal reframed the factory from a target into something closer to a heart, beating under stasis magic, with Captain Steel's presence a reminder that whatever the party is planning, someone senior enough to matter is already circling the same factory.

## Session MVP
**Elspeth Cooper**, for turning a supply run into an intelligence coup, a sticker heist, and — depending on how tomorrow's picnic collides with tomorrow's bombing run — possibly a complication the party will be untangling for sessions to come.

> *The Archive issues an amendment to this account. As first published, this recap placed Captain Steel's meeting inside the furnace chamber; it took place beside a corner office off an exit throughway, and it was Ms. Cooper, from the catwalk, who saw it. The same account credited Mr. Bru with a two-stage breach that Mr. Fairbanks proposed and Mr. Bru doubted, and with a build estimate he never gave; handed the meatloaf-cart remarks to a shift manager who does not exist, when they belonged to Ms. Goldspring and the factory administrator; recorded a sleight-of-hand check of 16 as an 8; charged Ms. Cooper for a purchase Scarlet paid, before a 20-gold discount the record had omitted; sent the party "back" into a factory it had never left; scheduled for "that night" a strike since moved to the morrow; had seventy-five stickers both spent and held in reserve; and listed three attendees who were not in the room. Eleven passages amended, one quotation respelled and another divided between its two speakers, and one summary line rewritten. This unit has identified the pattern — where a line could plausibly have come from someone nearby, the Archive assigned it to the nearest named party member — and has flagged it against future entries. — V.*

> *One counts eleven amendments and a single pattern, and the pattern is attribution: a machine hears a voice and hands the words to whichever name sits nearest in its tables.
